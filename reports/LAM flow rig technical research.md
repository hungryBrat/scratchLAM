# Evidence rewrites the flow rig's riskiest assumptions

The biggest single risk to the Stage 2 score is the upload, not the hardware. The LRC portal's Hardware Hustle form takes exactly three files: a **3-4 min Journey Video (MP4)**, a **10-12 min Challenge Video (MP4)** and a **scanned handwritten PDF in blue or black ink**. It has no code or photo field, and the submit is irreversible. The plan instead builds one 14-minute video plus a typed paper. On the bench, the evidence predicts three hydraulic surprises. **Pump-1 cannot pull 3.3 L/min through its 2.5 mm suction-side solenoid**, and raising the duty will not fix that. Small 12 V diaphragm pumps **lose about 15% of their flow by 0.7 bar and about half by cut-off**, so BV-02 has authority everywhere. And Case 1's "P-02 at 100%, BV-02 trimmed to 3.0 L/min" point sits on the valve's steep knee, near the pressure-switch cut-off. The YF-S401 can meet ≤3% with Q = a·f + b, but the real gate is ~1% run-to-run scatter, not R², and the calibration must be verified in the exact Case 1 configuration. The BTS7960 current sense is diagnostic grade, so the planned ADC range can saturate and a driver fault can drive the pin towards 9-12 V. The touch probe has ample contrast, but its 0.3 s trip and its scale across core versions are unverified.

## The portal wants three files, and the plan builds the wrong ones

The official site describes Hardware Hustle as an online October semi-final in which teams build a physical system ("**no simulations, no shortcuts**"), "acquire real data, characterise system performance, and validate results through calculations, plots", and 25 of 50 teams advance ([LRC 2026](https://lrc2026.tworks.in)). The team portal's code defines the deliverable: a Journey Video ("3–4 minute video showcasing your team's efforts and prototype development journey"), a Challenge Video ("10–12 minute video demonstrating the functional prototype") and a "Scanned handwritten solution in PDF format, written using blue or black ink". The form rejects anything but MP4 and PDF, offers a ZIP field only for other rounds, and warns "**You cannot change this after it is saved**" ([Team bundle](https://lrc2026.tworks.in/assets/Team-81254daf.js)). Evaluators score Hardware Hustle on the same marks table as the qualifier, with a default maximum of 120 and an unpublished split ([EvaluationDashboard bundle](https://lrc2026.tworks.in/assets/EvaluationDashboard-48d6b8bf.js)).

**12:00 by file duration is the hard cap**, because it satisfies both the paper's 10-15 min and the portal's 10-12. The storyboard runs to 14:00. Moving team roles and the build story into the journey video, and compressing the calibration and fault segments around the unbroken Case 1 → Case 2 take, fixes that. The paper's code, photographs and P&ID have no upload field, so they go inside the handwritten PDF as pasted pages (unconfirmed, so ask the organisers) and on a title card in the video, with the repository link tested in a logged-out browser. Re-encode phone footage (often HEVC or .mov) to H.264/AAC MP4, upload hours early from a laptop (login is "laptop or desktop only"), and have a second person check durations and page count before pressing Confirm. The public API's window ends **19 Oct** ([api/rounds](https://api.lrc2026.tworks.in/api/rounds)), but nothing says that is the upload deadline. Treat 4 Oct 18:00 IST as binding unless the dashboard's per-team countdown says otherwise.

The jury rubric in the site code, almost certainly for the Grand Finale, is the best public signal of what judges reward. It weights Technical Execution at 20%, including "Consistency of performance across trials". Integration, Troubleshooting (including "Failure Recovery") and Team Collaboration get 15% each. Project Management (including "Version Control") and Documentation (including "Decision Clarity") get 10% each ([Jury bundle](https://lrc2026.tworks.in/assets/Jury-ad9a3573.js)). A per-member rubric adds Integrity & Ethics ("Honesty", "Rule Respect") ([Observer bundle](https://lrc2026.tworks.in/assets/Observer-0710622c.js)). Past finales give nothing to copy for a fluid rig. 2024 was a **wafer handler**, judged on integration, hardware and firmware, cost and project management ([Business News This Week](https://businessnewsthisweek.com/business/lam-research-announces-winners-of-lam-systems-engineering-challenge/)). 2025 was an "integrated automation system", judged on "control logic, implementation quality, performance outcomes" ([LRC 2026](https://lrc2026.tworks.in)). Leftover portal strings, probably from 2025, include a simulation task, which suggests the 2026 ban is new; no public write-up of what winners did exists. The judging language has drifted towards **measured performance**: keep the simulator out of every deliverable, and show the spread across trials, not just the best run.

LRC publishes no editing rules. e-Yantra, the closest Indian precedent, requires the demo to be "**a one-shot continuous video**" at real speed, with the data screen and the hardware "recorded simultaneously", a mounted camera, landscape framing and no music. It also warns that a "sudden change in light might falsely lead one to believe editing of the video and hence, disqualification" ([eYRC theme book mirror](https://github.com/izzaaaatanishq/Hologlyph-Bots/tree/main/theme-book/4.%20Stage%202%20-%20Hardware/Task%206)). The plan's tripod take with the console picture-in-picture already fits these rules. Its correction of the paper's "Tr = Q/V" to V/Q is right.

## Pump-1 starves on its suction solenoid well short of 3.3 L/min

These pumps self-prime only 2-3 m. Shurflo rates the 8000 series to "8 vertical feet" ([Pentair](https://www.pentair.com/en-us/flow/commercial-products/water-supply-pumps/spray-pumps/8000_series_diaphgram_pumps.html)), so the realistic suction-side loss budget is **~0.3-0.5 bar**. A direct-acting 2.5 mm valve has Kv 0.16 ([Burkert 6014, Tameson](https://tameson.com/products/solenoid-valve-3-2-way-g1-4-no-brass-fkm-0-4bar-58psi-110vac-6014-312525)), and the plan's own figure is Cv 0.23 (Kv 0.20). Because ΔP = (Q/Kv)², a Kv 0.20 valve allows **1.8 L/min within 0.3 bar, 2.4 within 0.5 bar and 2.8 within 0.7 bar**. Pulsatile suction spikes ΔP above the mean-flow figure, and raising TK-01 by 0.3 m buys only ~0.03 bar. This contradicts the plan's remedy of raising d1. A starved pump plateaus as duty rises, draws low current, sounds gravelly, sends bubbles down the clear tube and makes FT-01 counts erratic. None of these are firmware faults. Pump manuals agree: the VEVOR 12 V manual asks for 13 mm inlet hoses, no restrictive fittings, and check valves cracking at ≤2 psi ([VEVOR manual](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/708a9dd0-5cd6-410b-b75f-74d3866cf05c/88568163.pdf)).

Case 1 needs P-01 to out-pump P-02 by ~10%, so **P-01's measured ceiling, not 3.0 L/min, sets the loop target**. The fixes, in order: move SOV-01 to P-01's discharge, if the paper's P&ID allows it; fit a 4-6 mm direct-acting valve (Kv ~0.4-0.8), not a pilot-operated one, which needs differential pressure to open; or take the plan's last resort and document the reduced target. Run the M1 duty-finder test before anything else, and measure SOV-01's real Kv from ΔP at a known flow.

The pumps also droop far more than "positive displacement" implies. A Shurflo 8000-543-238 (12 V, 3-chamber, permanent-magnet motor) gives **6.8 L/min open, 5.8 at 0.69 bar (−15%) and 3.6 at 6.9 bar (−47%)**, while current rises from 3.0 A to 8.7 A. Motor speed falls only 14-16%, so most of the lost flow is internal leakage ([Shurflo data sheet](http://www.depcopump.com/datasheets/shurflo/8000-543-238.pdf)). Scaled to a 6.1 L/min pump, that is ~4.9 L/min at 1.4 bar, and cheap sprayer pumps are likely leakier. Internal-bypass variants collapse to 0.7 L/min near their setting while current stays high ([Shurflo catalogue](https://www.prumyslove-trysky.cz/dokumenty/shurflo-8000.pdf)), so check the pump head for a second adjustment screw. A demand switch with ~1 bar of hysteresis short-cycles at cut-off, which shows up as a square on/off current train. At reduced PWM a pump may stall against a closed valve instead of reaching cut-off, which is the VEVOR manual's "insufficient voltage" failure, so the plan's I-4 trip should key on "high current, no pulses".

PWM at 20 kHz suits these brushed motors, and the BTS7960 is rated to 25 kHz ([BTS7960 datasheet](https://www.robotpower.com/downloads/BTS7960_v1.1_2004-12-07.pdf)). Flow is not proportional to duty, though. One forum report gives no rotation below ~40% duty and start-up current "several times" the running current ([All About Circuits](https://forum.allaboutcircuits.com/threads/speed-control-and-on-off-switch-for-12v-dc-motor.145333/post-1234815)). Expect a dead zone, a start duty above the minimum sustaining duty, and separate Q(duty) lines for each pump. A brief full-duty kick before the ramp starts more reliably than a ramp from zero, and the FIC duty map's 30% point may not turn at all. Also confirm the pumps are plain brushed units: one "pump with speed dial" turned out to be a BLDC with its own controller and failed under PWM ([Arduino forum](https://forum.arduino.cc/t/problems-with-pump-speed-control/445205)).

## BV-02's knee spans about 18 degrees, and Case 1 sits on it

A round-bore ball valve barely opens until late in its travel. Its loss coefficient is **K = 0.05 fully open, 5.5 at one-third closed and 200 at two-thirds closed** ([Engineering ToolBox](https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/minor-loss-coefficients-pipes-d_626.html)), and at 50% travel it has only **7.3% of its maximum Cv** ([Valmet T2H table](https://www.valmet.com/globalassets/flow-control/services/T2_T2H.html)). Combining those losses with the scaled Shurflo curve, 2 m of 5 mm tube, four fittings and the YF-S401 gives this modelled installed characteristic at 100% duty:

| Handle open | 100% | 75% | 70% | 60% | 50% | 40% | 30% | 25% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flow (L/min) | 4.8 | 4.7 | 4.7 | 4.5 | 4.3 | 3.7 | 2.7 | switch cycles |
| Pump pressure (bar) | 1.7 | 1.8 | 1.9 | 2.3 | 3.5 | 5.7 | 8.2 | >8.25 cut-off |

The shape the plan expects is right, but two of its claims are wrong: that the valve "has little authority until it dominates", and that the plateau sits at the pump's rating. Flow falls ~0.15-0.3 L/min for every bar the valve adds, and the steep part fits into ~18° of handle travel. The paper's 70% and 75% points will sit within 2% of each other, and the 25% hold will probably cycle the switch, giving a mean that depends on the averaging window. A smaller mini valve (Cv_max 1.5) reaches cut-off earlier, at ~35-40% open. Add 40% and ~33% to resolve the knee, and log IT-02, which rises ~0.8 A per bar on the Shurflo curve and works as a free pressure gauge.

For Case 1, interpolating the table puts **3.0 L/min at 100% duty near one-third open and ~7 bar**: the steepest part of the curve, within about a bar of the 8.25 bar cut-off, with current at 2-3× the open-flow value. Small handle drift moves the flow noticeably, and pump ripple could touch the switch. Set the trim and check that IT-02 is steady rather than an on/off train. If the paper allows it, setting 3.0 L/min with P-02's duty and BV-02 open keeps the pump on the flat, low-pressure part of the curve. Ask alongside the existing duty question.

Pipe friction is not the bottleneck. In 5 mm bore tubing, flow is turbulent at all three set-points (Re ≈ 4,750 / 14,300 / 28,500), with losses of **~0.027 / 0.18 / 0.62 bar per metre** at 1 / 3 / 6 L/min. The small bores do the damage: the YF-S401's 4 mm ports ([DFRobot SEN0216](https://wiki.dfrobot.com/SEN0216)), the push-fits and the mini valve add an estimated 2-4 bar at 6 L/min, where the pump curve gives only ~4.2-4.6 L/min. **6 L/min at the meter is very unlikely.**

A dye front or bubble nose rides near the centreline, which runs at ~1.22× the mean for a 1/7 power-law profile ([Cambridge](https://www-mdp.eng.cam.ac.uk/web/library/enginfo/aerothermal_dvd_only/aero/fprops/pipeflow/node22.html)). Taylor bubbles move at ~1.2× the mean in turbulent flow ([arXiv 2109.09091](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.09091)). The standard method times the **centroids** of the tracer curves at two stations ([USBR](https://www.usbr.gov/tsc/techreferences/mands/wmm/chap12_06.html)). A front speed 20-30% above Q/A is the expected physics, not an error. At 3 L/min, 1.00 m takes 0.39 s, so use 240 fps video or a 3-5 m coil.

## FT-01 is linear only with a free intercept, and only where it was calibrated

The YF-S401 datasheets agree on the pulse constant (**5,880 pulses/L, F = 98·Q, 0.17 mL/pulse**) and little else. Stated accuracy is ±2% ([iarduino PDF](https://iarduino.ru/lib/2377bf7550064248856024f6728d7cc3.pdf)), ±5% over 0.3-3 L/min only ([DFRobot](https://wiki.dfrobot.com/SEN0216)), or ±10% ([TinyTronics](https://www.tinytronics.nl/shop/en/sensors/liquid/yf-s401-water-flow-sensor)), and the range and pressure figures conflict too. The team's own calibration is the only defensible number. "2.25 mL per pulse" snippets describe the YF-S201.

The pulses-per-litre figure is not constant. datalogd's published YF-S401 fit has K rising from **~5,140 p/L at 1 L/min to ~5,735 at 5 L/min**, and its author says each pulse "does not correspond to a fixed volume" ([datalogd](https://datalogd.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/datalogd.plugins.flowsensorcalibration_datafilter.html)). Nearly all of this is offset. Fitting Q = a·f + b to that curve over 1-5 L/min gives 1/a ≈ 98 Hz per L/min, **b ≈ +0.14 L/min** and a maximum error of 1.2%, whereas a fit forced through the origin is 9% off at 1 L/min. Adding a 0.5 L/min point raises the error there to 4.6%. The plan's model and its "b is physical" note hold: K = 60/a should land within ~±10% of 5,880, and a larger miss means air, wiring or a miscounted volume. Fit only from 1 L/min up.

Pulsation is the subtle risk. Small turbine meters **over-register by up to 5.5%** in pulsating flow, because the rotor speeds up faster than it slows down ([Brunel thesis](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/handle/2438/4971?mode=full)). These pumps pulse at ~100-180 Hz. In-place calibration absorbs a *constant* bias, but the plan calibrates with P-02's duty varied and the valve open (`CAL 60 35`), then runs Case 1 at 100% duty with BV-02 throttled just upstream of FT-01. Stroke frequency, pulsation damping and the inlet jet all differ between the two. Meter practice is blunt: "**Throttling valves should be installed downstream of the turbine flow meter only**" ([Turbines Inc. LF manual](https://testing.instrumart.com/assets/turbines-inc-lf-series-low-flow-turbine-meter-Manual.pdf)), and the attached tubing shifts Hall-meter calibration ([IEEE 8307842](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8307842/)). FT-01's placement after BV-02 protects its 0.8 MPa rating, so keep it. Leave ≥10 cm of straight tube between them, and **run the ±2% verification at the exact Case 1 setting**. If it fails, add three calibration runs there.

For the weighing, the dominant gravimetric error is the start/stop transient, not the scale ([Sârbu, IMEKO 2017](https://imeko.org/publications/tc4-2017/IMEKO-TC4-2017-093.pdf)). The plan already counts until the rotor stops, shares one ESP32 window between pulses and mass, and drains the return line the same way each time (8×5 tube holds 19.6 mL/m). One free correction is missing. Water density is **998.2 / 997.0 / 995.7 kg/m³ at 20 / 25 / 30 °C** ([Engineering ToolBox](https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/water-density-specific-weight-d_595.html)), and air buoyancy adds 0.105%, so the 1000 g/L default under-states volume by **~0.48% at 28 °C**. Use V = m·1.00105/ρ(T). Running 120 s (2 kg) rather than 90 s at 1 L/min halves the fixed per-run errors. ISO 4185's long-versus-split-run test measures the per-start/stop error directly ([PTB](https://www.imeko.org/publications/tc9-2013/IMEKO-TC9-2013-013.pdf)).

R² cannot be the gate. It "did a poor job of consistently and accurately quantifying" calibration fit ([Logue 2018](https://openprairie.sdstate.edu/chem_pubs/77)), and the highest-R² dataset can be the worst ([Coleman & Vanatta](https://americanlaboratory.com/913-Technical-Articles/35232-Part-44-R-sup-2-sup/)). A Monte Carlo of the planned 5 × 3 design found that **2% per-run scatter passes R² ≥ 0.995 (median 0.9983) while giving a 3.9% maximum error**. Meeting ≤3% needs ~1% scatter, so "repeats within ±1%" is the binding test. Lead the certificate with residuals in % of reading and the independent 3 L/min check, and put R² last.

## The current sense is a fault detector, and the touch probe depends on the core version

The BTS7960's sense ratio kILIS is nominally 8,500, but it is guaranteed only within **3,000-14,000 at 5 A**, with nothing specified below 5 A. Zero-current leakage can reach 200 µA, and any fault turns IS into a fixed **4-7 mA** source ([BTS7960 datasheet](https://www.robotpower.com/downloads/BTS7960_v1.1_2004-12-07.pdf)). One IBT-2 user's measurements imply k ≈ 6,100-6,500 ([OpenMarine forum](https://forum.openmarine.net/printthread.php?tid=3571)). The plan's ~0.24 V/A into a 2.5 dB ADC (calibrated **100-1250 mV, ±30 mV** ([ESP32 datasheet](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32_datasheet_en.pdf))) gives a nominal 5.2 A full scale. A low-k part saturates near 1.9 A, and the throttled Case 1 P-02 may exceed even the nominal range, which would blind the IT-02 RESTRICTION diagnosis. Below 100 mV readings are dead: 1 A at 40% duty averages 94 mV. After the planned per-channel DMM calibration, consider 11 dB (150-2450 mV, ±60 mV) for headroom, and treat a pinned full-scale reading as a driver fault.

A sustained fault presents ~9-12 V behind ~12 kΩ, injecting 0.45-0.7 mA into a chip that specifies VIH max = VDD + 0.3 V and no injection current at all. Add a BAT54 from each ADC node to 3V3, and measure the unconfirmed on-board IS resistors before choosing the added resistor. The rest of the pin plan holds. G15 is ADC2, which Wi-Fi blocks ([ESP-IDF ADC](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v4.4.7/esp32/api-reference/peripherals/adc.html)), and as a strapping pin it at most silences the boot log. The GPIO36/39 80 ns erratum ([ESP32 errata](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-chip-errata/en/latest/esp32/03-errata-description/esp32/gpio-inputs-pulled-down.html)) does not affect ADC reads behind 100 nF.

The ESP32 touch reading *falls* as capacitance rises ([ESP-IDF touch_pad](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v4.4.7/esp32/api-reference/peripherals/touch_pad.html)). A bare wire in water drops from **~100 to ~10** in one maker's build ([Hackaday.io](https://hackaday.io/project/186829/log/211790)) and from 560 to 360 in another ([Node-RED forum](https://discourse.nodered.org/t/cheap-water-level-sensor/22060)). The firmware's midpoint threshold between captured wet and dry readings (tchF = 0.5) is right; a threshold at 90-95% of dry would false-trip. Still unverified is whether a meniscus bridging the tinned tips 5 mm apart holds "wet" after the water has gone, so cut the tips square, keep the tape back, and time the **dry-after-wet** transition. Espressif says the touch sensor "has not passed the Conducted Susceptibility (CS) test" ([ESP32 datasheet](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32_datasheet_en.pdf)). Tie the probe's GND wire to logic ground, never motor ground, and route the lead away from the 20 kHz motor wires.

The LEDC break in core 3.x is documented ([migration guide](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/arduino-esp32/en/latest/migration_guides/2.x_to_3.0.html)), and the firmware already guards it. The touch break is undocumented. From core 3.3 (ESP-IDF 5.5) the ESP32 uses a new driver: `touch_value_t` becomes uint32_t, `touchSetCycles` is gone, reads return filtered "SMOOTH" data on a different scale, and the first read runs calibration scans ([esp32-hal-touch-ng.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/master/cores/esp32/esp32-hal-touch-ng.c)). The filter's lag is undocumented. Builds on 2.0.17 and 3.1.3 use the legacy driver and are safe. But platformio.ini points 3.x users to pioarduino "stable", which pulls 3.3 or later and silently changes both the touch scale and the trip delay. Pin the core, check the version the firmware prints at boot before the take, and re-run TOUCHCAL on that exact build.

## Conclusion

The evidence moves the main risk away from the firmware, which the simulator has already tested, to two things the simulator cannot reach: physics at the pump inlet and the valve knee, and the submission's format. Both can be settled within hours. The P-01 duty-finder test and one BV-02 sweep with IT-02 logged will show in the first rig hour whether the loop target, the Q2.1 answer and the Case 1 trim need to change. The three-file reformat is pure editing, but the storyboard overruns by two minutes and the submit is final, so plan it now. What remains uncertain (this pump's droop and bypass, SOV-01's Kv, each IBT-2's k, the probe's dry-after-wet time, LRC's editing rules) can be measured on the bench or answered by the organisers.

The deviations are an asset on video. Suction starvation, pump droop, calibration bias and current saturation are the paper's own "detect a deviation, diagnose its cause, and restore", and the rubric pays for "Root Analysis", "Failure Recovery" and "Decision Clarity". Showing P-01 hitting its suction ceiling, explaining it with a Kv calculation and documenting the corrected target will score better than hiding the shortfall behind a raised duty and a single best take.
