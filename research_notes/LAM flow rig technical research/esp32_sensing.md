# ESP32 sensing for the LAM flow rig: touch-pin water probe, ADC limits, BTS7960 current sense

## Q1. ESP32 touch sensor as a bare-wire water-level probe (air vs immersed, conductivity, GND electrode, leads, PWM noise, drift)

### Takeaway
On the original ESP32 the touch reading falls when the electrode's capacitance rises; a bare wire dipped in water drops hard (reported ~100 -> ~10, i.e. ~90 %, on Arduino defaults; ~560 -> ~360 on MicroPython), so wet vs dry is a large, easy margin. The weak points are not the wet/dry contrast but (a) absolute drift with temperature, humidity, supply and lead routing, which makes a once-captured dry reading a fragile reference, (b) a water film or droplet left on the wire tips after the level falls, and (c) on core 3.3+ the API, scale and filtering all changed.

### Cited Findings
- Measurement principle: the ESP32 counts charge/discharge cycles in a fixed measurement window; "the raw value will decrease as the capacity of the touch pad increasing". Touch pads T0-T9 = GPIO4, 0, 2, 15, 13, 12, 14, 27, 33, 32. Max measure time 0xffff / 8 MHz = 8.19 ms. — [ESP-IDF v4.4 touch_pad (ESP32)](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v4.4.7/esp32/api-reference/peripherals/touch_pad.html)
- Espressif warns that hardware touch detection "might be unreliable" when measurements are noisy and the capacitance change is small, and that temperature, humidity and supply-voltage changes all shift readings; it offers an IIR filter (touch_pad_filter_start) and recommends software detection. — [ESP-IDF v4.4 touch_pad (ESP32)](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v4.4.7/esp32/api-reference/peripherals/touch_pad.html)
- Arduino doc: on ESP32 the interrupt fires when the value "falls below the given threshold" (on S2/S3 it rises above). — [Arduino-ESP32 Touch API](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/arduino-esp32/en/latest/api/touch.html)
- Maker report (Arduino, tank level, two bare wires at two heights): touchRead gives "a value between 100 and 0. By dipping a wire into the water the value will drop to around 10". The build used a 15 s fill timeout and polled the low sensor every 2 min (slow, not a fast-trip use). — [Hackaday.io project 186829 log](https://hackaday.io/project/186829/log/211790)
- Forum report (MicroPython, single wire to a metal barrel adapter as electrode, no separate GND wire): "I get a reading of 560 in free air and 360 in water", threshold set at 500. — [Node-RED forum, Cheap water level sensor](https://discourse.nodered.org/t/cheap-water-level-sensor/22060)
- Same thread: "Water can be surprisingly non-conductive just when you need it not to be. Especially if the contacts have any possibility of getting oxidised." — [Node-RED forum](https://discourse.nodered.org/t/cheap-water-level-sensor/22060)
- User comparison: an ESP32 pad read ~76 idle and ~7 touched (Arduino 2.x scale), whereas ESP32-S3 reads ~16,300 idle rising to ~800,000; scales and directions differ by chip. — [esp32.com p=130231](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?p=130231) (via search snippet)
- Users report the touchRead scale "changes drastically, differently for different channels", so a threshold must be per-pin and per-build. — [arduino-esp32 issue 5109](https://github.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/issues/5109) (via search snippet)
- Arduino core 3.3+ (ESP-IDF >= 5.5) uses a new touch driver (esp32-hal-touch-ng). On ESP32 it reads TOUCH_CHAN_DATA_TYPE_SMOOTH (software-filtered) data, not raw; defaults in source: `_duration_ms = 5.0f`, `_volt_low = TOUCH_VOLT_LIM_L_0V5`, `_volt_high = TOUCH_VOLT_LIM_H_1V7`; the first touchRead on a pad runs init and a benchmark ("three calibration scans"). — [esp32-hal-touch-ng.c (master)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/master/cores/esp32/esp32-hal-touch-ng.c)

### Inferences
- With a GND wire 5 mm away in tap water, the electrode is no longer just a capacitor to the surroundings: conductive tap water gives a resistive path to GND that loads the pad, so the immersed reading should be pulled very low, near the ~10 floor reported above. A GND wire 5 mm away therefore increases contrast. The GND wire is not needed for detection (single-wire reports work), but it makes the result less dependent on tank/body capacitance.
- Plan assumption "trips reliably within 0.3 s once the water drops 2-3 mm below the wire tips": the sensing itself is fast (0.5 ms per read on 2.x defaults; a few ms on 3.3), but the physical risk is a meniscus or droplet bridging the two tinned tips 5 mm apart, or a wet film on tape around them. That holds the reading low ("still wet") after the bulk water has gone. Tinned stranded wire wicks water. Mitigation: cut the tips square, point them down, keep the tape back from the tips, and do not bridge the gap with tape. Check the dry-after-wet reading on the bench, not only the dry-before-wet one.
- A dry reading captured once by command will drift with temperature, humidity, supply rail sag under motor load, and any move of the lead. Set the threshold as a fraction of the dry value. Because the wet/dry contrast is so large (about 90 % drop on 2.x), a threshold near 50 % of dry gives a wide margin either way; a threshold at 90-95 % of dry would false-trip.
- Long unshielded leads add capacitance to the pad and pick up coupled noise. Lead capacitance lowers the dry reading (it acts like a partial touch), shrinking the dry/wet ratio. Motor wires running alongside at 20 kHz PWM can couple noise; route the probe lead away from the motor and BTS7960 output wires, and take a median of N reads.
- On core 3.3+ (ESP-IDF 5.5 cap_touch_sens driver), ESP32 v1 smooth data comes from a software filter run periodically by esp_timer (touch_sensor_filter_config_t::interval_ms); the docs give no default IIR coefficients. Per the [ESP-IDF v5.5 capacitive touch docs](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v5.5/esp32/api-reference/peripherals/cap_touch_sens.html), the ESP32 "channel value decreases when it is touched" in this driver too. The SMOOTH data path adds filter lag, so the trip delay depends on the IDF filter settings. Measure it on the bench.

### Gaps
- No controlled published measurement found of ESP32 touch counts vs water conductivity, lead length, or with a GND electrode at a set spacing. The two maker data points (100 -> 10 and 560 -> 360) differ in core/config and electrode, so they only bound the effect.
- No source found quantifying the meniscus/droplet bridging time for two wires 5 mm apart; it must be bench-tested (the plan's 0.3 s claim is unverified).
- Filter time constant of TOUCH_SENSOR_DEFAULT_FILTER_CONFIG for ESP32 v1 under IDF 5.5 not confirmed, so the response delay on core 3.3+ is unknown.
- No reliable data found on through-wall capacitive sensing with the ESP32 on a 4.2 L plastic tank; it is not the plan's method and was not pursued.

## Q2. Arduino-ESP32 core 2.0.17 vs 3.x API differences (touchRead, ADC, LEDC)

### Takeaway
LEDC is the guaranteed compile break: ledcSetup/ledcAttachPin are gone in 3.x (use ledcAttach(pin, freq, res) and ledcWrite(pin, duty)). The touch API is a hidden second break: from core 3.3 (ESP-IDF 5.5) the ESP32 build uses a new touch driver where touch_value_t becomes uint32_t, touchSetCycles no longer exists (touchSetTiming/touchSetConfig instead), and the value scale and filtering differ, so dry/wet thresholds captured on one core are not valid on another. The ADC calls and attenuation enum names (ADC_0db, ADC_2_5db, ADC_6db, ADC_11db) are the same in both.

### Cited Findings
- Official migration guide 2.x -> 3.0, LEDC: `ledcSetup` and `ledcAttachPin` removed; new `ledcAttach` "merged `ledcSetup` and `ledcAttachPin` functions"; `ledcDetachPin` renamed `ledcDetach`; "In all functions, input parameter `channel` has been changed to `pin`". ADC: `analogSetClockDiv`, `adcAttachPin`, `analogSetVRefPin` removed. The guide says nothing about touch changes. — [Arduino-ESP32 migration guide 2.x to 3.0](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/arduino-esp32/en/latest/migration_guides/2.x_to_3.0.html)
- Legacy touch header (used when ESP-IDF < 5.5 and SOC_TOUCH_SENSOR_VERSION <= 2, i.e. core 2.0.17 and 3.0-3.2): `typedef uint16_t touch_value_t;` on ESP32; `void touchSetCycles(uint16_t measure, uint16_t sleep);` "Defaults are 0x1000 for measure and 0x1000 for sleep. With default values touchRead takes 0.5ms". ESP32-only `touchInterruptSetThresholdDirection(bool mustbeLower)`. — [esp32-hal-touch.h (master)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/master/cores/esp32/esp32-hal-touch.h)
- New touch header guarded by `#if ESP_IDF_VERSION >= ESP_IDF_VERSION_VAL(5, 5, 0) || SOC_TOUCH_SENSOR_VERSION == 3` covers ESP32 too: `typedef uint32_t touch_value_t;`, no `touchSetCycles`; adds `touchSetTiming` (time in us, "must be called before setting up touch pads"), `touchSetConfig` (duration_ms on ESP32), `touchSetDefaultThreshold` ("percentage of the benchmark value. The default value is 1.5%"). — [esp32-hal-touch-ng.h (master)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/master/cores/esp32/esp32-hal-touch-ng.h)
- In the new driver, touchSetTiming after init logs "Touch sensor already initialized. Cannot set cycles." and returns. — [esp32-hal-touch-ng.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/master/cores/esp32/esp32-hal-touch-ng.c)
- ADC header (master): `typedef enum { ADC_0db, ADC_2_5db, ADC_6db, ADC_11db, ADC_ATTENDB_MAX } adc_attenuation_t;`; `uint16_t analogRead(uint8_t pin); uint32_t analogReadMilliVolts(uint8_t pin); void analogSetPinAttenuation(uint8_t pin, adc_attenuation_t attenuation);` `analogSetWidth` only on ESP32; default attenuation 11 dB. — [esp32-hal-adc.h (master)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/master/cores/esp32/esp32-hal-adc.h)
- Arduino ADC doc gives ESP32 ranges: ADC_0db 100-950 mV, ADC_2_5db 100-1250 mV, ADC_6db 150-1750 mV, ADC_11db 150-3100 mV; analogReadResolution on ESP32 changes hardware resolution between 9 and 12 bits. — [Arduino-ESP32 ADC API](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/arduino-esp32/en/latest/api/adc.html)

### Inferences
- Portable LEDC: `#if ESP_ARDUINO_VERSION_MAJOR >= 3` -> `ledcAttach(pin, 20000, res); ledcWrite(pin, d);` else `ledcSetup(ch, 20000, res); ledcAttachPin(pin, ch); ledcWrite(ch, d);`. At 20 kHz the 80 MHz APB clock allows up to floor(log2(80e6/20e3)) = 11 bits, so use 10 bits (or at most 11).
- Portable touch: guard touchSetCycles with `#if ESP_ARDUINO_VERSION < ESP_ARDUINO_VERSION_VAL(3,3,0)` (or simply never call it), store readings in uint32_t, and set thresholds as a ratio of the captured dry value, never as an absolute number, because the scale differs between cores.
- ADC attenuation: ADC_2_5db compiles on both cores unchanged.

### Gaps
- The migration guide does not document the 3.3 touch rewrite. The facts above come from the current header and source on master, not from release notes; the exact release that switched (assumed 3.3.0, the first core on ESP-IDF 5.5) was not confirmed from a changelog.
- Typical ESP32 touchRead magnitudes on core 3.3+ (new driver) were not found.
- Not verified in this pass: whether PlatformIO's official espressif32 platform still ships core 2.0.17 and needs the community pioarduino platform for 3.x (believed so). Check which core `pio run` actually builds against, because the touch scale differs.

## Q3. ESP32 ADC accuracy, attenuation ranges, calibration, ADC2/Wi-Fi conflict, GPIO36/39 erratum, strapping pins

### Takeaway
The ESP32 ADC is usable for a 1-4 A current reading only inside its calibrated window. At 2.5 dB that window is 100-1250 mV, with a total error of up to +/-30 mV after eFuse calibration. Inputs below about 100 mV fall into a dead zone, so low duty or low current will read as zero or badly nonlinear. The GPIO36/39 erratum is an 80 ns pull-down glitch that matters for digital interrupts, not for ADC use with a 100 nF cap. GPIO15 is a strapping pin (MTDO, boot-log control) whose only practical effect here is a silent boot log if the IS network pulls it low at reset. GPIO12 must never be high at reset on a WROOM-32.

### Cited Findings
- Datasheet ADC characteristics (RTC controller, external 100 nF on the input, DC input, 25 C, Wi-Fi and BT off): DNL -7..+7 LSB, INL -12..+12 LSB; up to 200 ksps (RTC) / 2 Msps (DIG). "When atten = 3 and the measurement result is above 3000 (voltage at approx. 2450 mV), the ADC accuracy will be worse." — [ESP32 Series Datasheet v5.3, Table 4-3](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32_datasheet_en.pdf)
- "By default, there are ±6% differences in measured results between chips." Total error after eFuse-Vref calibration: atten 0 (100-950 mV) +/-23 mV; atten 1 / 2.5 dB (100-1250 mV) +/-30 mV; atten 2 / 6 dB (150-1750 mV) +/-40 mV; atten 3 / 11-12 dB (150-2450 mV) +/-60 mV. — [ESP32 Series Datasheet v5.3, Table 4-4](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32_datasheet_en.pdf)
- GPIO input high level max is VDD + 0.3 V (about 3.6 V at 3.3 V); supply absolute max 3.6 V. — [ESP32 Series Datasheet v5.3, Tables 5-1 and 5-3](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32_datasheet_en.pdf)
- ESP-IDF ranges: DB_0 100-950 mV, DB_2_5 100-1250 mV, DB_6 150-1750 mV, DB_12 (formerly DB_11) 150-2450 mV. Calibration sources: Two Point (150 mV and 850 mV readings), eFuse Vref, or Default Vref 1100 mV; chips made after Jan 2018 typically have eFuse Vref. — [ESP-IDF v4.4 ADC (ESP32)](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v4.4.7/esp32/api-reference/peripherals/adc.html)
- The Arduino ADC doc lists ADC_11db as 150-3100 mV, wider than the IDF/datasheet calibrated 150-2450 mV; above about 2450 mV accuracy degrades. — [Arduino-ESP32 ADC API](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/arduino-esp32/en/latest/api/adc.html) vs [ESP32 datasheet](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32_datasheet_en.pdf)
- Noise: "Users may connect a bypass capacitor (e.g. a 100 nF ceramic capacitor) to the ADC input pad in use, to minimize noise. Besides, multisampling may also be used." — [ESP-IDF v4.4 ADC](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v4.4.7/esp32/api-reference/peripherals/adc.html)
- ADC2/Wi-Fi: "Since the ADC2 module is also used by the Wi-Fi, only one of them could get the preemption when using together, which means the adc2_get_raw() may get blocked until Wi-Fi stops, and vice versa." The doc also says GPIO0, 2 and 15 cannot be freely used because of external connections on dev kits. — [ESP-IDF v4.4 ADC](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v4.4.7/esp32/api-reference/peripherals/adc.html)
- Erratum (GPIO-3.11): when SAR ADC1, SAR ADC2 or AMP is powered on, GPIO36 and GPIO39 inputs are pulled down for about 80 ns. Affects ESP32 v0.0, v1.0, v1.1, v3.0, v3.1; no fix scheduled; workaround is to ignore GPIO36/39 input at those moments, or call sar_periph_ctrl_adc_oneshot_power_acquire() (about 1 mA more). — [ESP32 errata: GPIO inputs pulled down](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-chip-errata/en/latest/esp32/03-errata-description/esp32/gpio-inputs-pulled-down.html); same note in [ESP-IDF v4.4 ADC](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v4.4.7/esp32/api-reference/peripherals/adc.html)
- Strapping pins and defaults: GPIO0 pull-up, GPIO2 pull-down, MTDI (GPIO12) pull-down, MTDO (GPIO15) pull-up, GPIO5 pull-up; latched at reset. MTDI = 1 at reset selects the 1.8 V VDD_SDIO LDO (default 0 = 3.3 V). MTDO = 0 disables U0TXD boot printing; MTDO with GPIO5 also sets SDIO-slave timing. GPIO15 = ADC2_CH3 / TOUCH3 / JTAG MTDO; GPIO12 = ADC2_CH5 / TOUCH5 / JTAG MTDI. — [ESP32 Series Datasheet v5.3, Section 3 and pin table](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32_datasheet_en.pdf)
- Internal weak pull-up/pull-down drive is about 75 uA. — [ESP32 Series Datasheet v5.3, IO_MUX notes](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32_datasheet_en.pdf)

### Inferences
- GPIO15 with the plan's network (2 kOhm load to GND, then 10 kOhm series, 100 nF to GND at the pin): at reset the pin sees about 12 kOhm to GND against a weak ~75 uA (~44 kOhm-equivalent) internal pull-up, so it most likely latches 0, which only silences the ROM boot log on UART0. Boot still works, because boot mode is set by GPIO0/GPIO2. Side effect: the ROM boot log, often used for debugging, disappears. JTAG is unaffected unless used.
- GPIO12: a WROOM-32 has 3.3 V flash. Anything that pulls GPIO12 high at reset gives 1.8 V flash and a boot loop or brownout. Keep it unused, or make sure it is low at reset.
- GPIO36/39 for the ADC: the 80 ns glitch is irrelevant to analog reads, because the 100 nF cap barely moves in 80 ns. Do not use GPIO36/39 as interrupt inputs (e.g. a flow-meter pulse) while the ADC runs.
- 2.5 dB attenuation and the 2 kOhm IS load: full scale 1250 mV corresponds to 0.625 mA of IS current (about 5.3 A of load current at k = 8500). The low end is the problem: below about 100 mV (0.05 mA IS, about 0.43 A load current at full duty) the reading is in the dead zone. A PWM-averaged reading (duty x I) drops into it sooner: at 1 A and 40 % duty the average is about 94 mV.
- An ADC-only error budget at 1 A (235 mV on 2 kOhm at 100 % duty): +/-30 mV calibrated error is about +/-13 %, before the BTS7960's own +/-20-ish % kILIS spread (see Q4). Use analogReadMilliVolts (eFuse-calibrated) and a per-rig two-point calibration against a multimeter.
- Wi-Fi off plus Bluetooth off makes GPIO15 ADC2 reads dependable. The IDF text names only Wi-Fi as the ADC2 conflict. Keep Wi-Fi off throughout the demo.

### Gaps
- The IDF doc consulted does not say whether Bluetooth alone blocks ADC2 on ESP32; only Wi-Fi is named.
- Exact behaviour of analogReadMilliVolts at the low end (whether it clamps to about 100 mV or returns 0) on core 2.0.17 vs 3.x was not verified from source.

## Q4. BTS7960 / IBT-2 IS current sense (kILIS, spread, offset, fault level, PWM behaviour, module resistors, practical accuracy)

### Takeaway
The BTS7960 IS output is a diagnostic-grade current mirror, not a measurement-grade sensor. kILIS is nominally 8500, but the datasheet guarantees only 3,000-14,000 at 5 A (5-12 k at 15 A, 6-11 k at 30 A), and nothing below 5 A. The zero-current leakage with the high side on can be up to 200 uA, which on 2 kOhm is up to 0.4 V, about 1.7 A equivalent at nominal k. A fault drives a fixed 4-7 mA, which on 2 kOhm wants 8-14 V, clamped only by VS. So the plan's IS network can hold the ESP32 pin well above 3.3 V during a sustained fault (latched overtemperature, overvoltage, repeated current limiting); a single current-limit flag mostly reads as a false spike. Per-unit calibration against a meter is mandatory, and the reading is fit for "pump running / stalled / dry-running" discrimination and trend, not for a precise number of amps at 1-4 A.

### Cited Findings
- Principle: "In normal operation (current sense mode), a current source is connected to the status pin, which delivers a current proportional to the forward load current flowing through the active high side switch. If the high side switch is inactive or the current is flowing in the reverse direction no current will be driven except for a marginal leakage current IIS(LK)." Example: kILIS = IL/IIS = 8500 nominal; RIS = 1 kOhm gives VIS = IL / 8.5 A volts. "In case of a fault condition the status output is connected to a current source which is independent of the load current and provides IIS(lim). The maximum voltage at the IS pin is determined by the choice of the external resistor and the supply voltage. In case of current limitation the IIS(lim) is activated for 2 * tCLS." — [Infineon BTS7960 datasheet Rev 1.1 (2004-12-07), Sec. 4.4.4, mirror at robotpower.com](https://www.robotpower.com/downloads/BTS7960_v1.1_2004-12-07.pdf)
- kILIS (x10^3, static on-condition, RIS = 1 kOhm, -40 C < Tj < 150 C, 8 V < VS < 18 V): IL = 30 A: 6 / 8.5 / 11 (min/typ/max); IL = 15 A: 5 / 8.5 / 12; IL = 5 A: 3 / 8.5 / 14. No values are given below 5 A. — [BTS7960 datasheet, item 4.4.6](https://www.robotpower.com/downloads/BTS7960_v1.1_2004-12-07.pdf)
- IIS(lim) (fault current): min 4 / typ 4.5 / max 7 mA at VS = 13.5 V, RIS = 1 kOhm. — [BTS7960 datasheet, item 4.4.7](https://www.robotpower.com/downloads/BTS7960_v1.1_2004-12-07.pdf)
- Leakage: IISL max 1 uA with the switch off (VIN = 0 or VINH = 0); IISH with the high side active and IL = 0 A: typ 1 uA, max 200 uA. — [BTS7960 datasheet, items 4.4.8-4.4.9](https://www.robotpower.com/downloads/BTS7960_v1.1_2004-12-07.pdf)
- Absolute max at IS: VIS -20 V to 45 V; VS - VIS -0.3 to 45 V. — [BTS7960 datasheet, items 3.0.7-3.0.8](https://www.robotpower.com/downloads/BTS7960_v1.1_2004-12-07.pdf)
- "PWM capability of up to 25 kHz combined with active freewheeling". High-side switching (RSR = 0 Ohm): rise/fall 0.5 / 1 / 1.5 us; switch-on delay 1.7 / 3.1 / 4.5 us; switch-off delay 1.2 / 2.4 / 3.6 us (RSR = 51 kOhm: on delay 5.6 / 14 / 22.4 us). No IS settling time is specified. — [BTS7960 datasheet, Sec. 1 and 4.2.3](https://www.robotpower.com/downloads/BTS7960_v1.1_2004-12-07.pdf)
- Current limit: high side 44 / 60 / 80 A, low side 34 / 43 / 61 A at 25 C; tCLS (shut-off time) 70 / 115 / 210 us. Fault flag held for 2 x tCLS. — [BTS7960 datasheet, Sec. 4.3.6-4.3.9](https://www.robotpower.com/downloads/BTS7960_v1.1_2004-12-07.pdf)
- Forum users restate the same nominal scale ("1 Volt on IS comes from 8.5 Amp output current" with 1 kOhm); one suggests about 8 kOhm to ground then a 2 kOhm series resistor to the Arduino; no measured accuracy reported. — [Arduino forum: BTS7960 current sensing](https://forum.arduino.cc/t/bts7960-current-sensing-how-to-use-it/973355)
- Handsontec's IBT-2 guide labels R_IS / L_IS as "side current alarm output" and leaves them unconnected in its example; it gives no on-board IS resistor values. — [Handsontec BTS7960 module doc](https://www.handsontec.com/dataspecs/module/BTS7960%20Motor%20Driver.pdf)
- Practical IBT-2 measurement (OpenMarine forum, user PaddyB, autopilot motor): with 1.2 kOhm from R_IS/L_IS to ground, "2.9A on load = 0.534v on IS pin"; with 470 Ohm, "3.34A = 0.258v". He chose to "calibrate to that". The pypilot author asked whether the output is linear; there was no answer. — [OpenMarine forum thread 3571](https://forum.openmarine.net/printthread.php?tid=3571)
- A pypilot/OpenMarine user wired "IBT_2 R_IS + L_IS --> 50kohm --> (Uno)A1 --> 10kohm --> GRND", and the pypilot author questioned it; pypilot itself uses its own shunt rather than IS. — [OpenMarine forum thread 1966](https://forum.openmarine.net/printthread.php?tid=1966)

### Inferences
- Plan scale (R_IS and L_IS tied, ~2 kOhm load): V = 2000 x IL / k. Nominal: 1 A -> 0.235 V, 2 A -> 0.47 V, 4 A -> 0.94 V (at 100 % duty). At the guaranteed k extremes (3 k-14 k at 5 A, which is likely wider at 1-4 A): 4 A gives 0.57-2.67 V. On a low-k part, the 2.5 dB range (1.25 V full scale) saturates near 1.9 A. FLAG: 2.5 dB attenuation is only safe for the nominal part. Either use 11/12 dB (150-2450 mV calibrated) or drop the load to about 1 kOhm and keep 2.5-6 dB, after measuring the actual unit.
- FLAG (pin safety): overvoltage, overtemperature (latched shutdown) and overcurrent turn IS into a 4-7 mA source. Into 2 kOhm that wants 8-14 V, limited only by VS (12 V rail). The plan's network seen from the pin is a Thevenin source of about 9 V (typ) behind 2 k + 10 k = 12 k into 100 nF, so tau is about 1.2 ms. (a) A single current-limit flag (2 x tCLS = 140-420 us) charges the cap only to about 9 x (1 - e^(-0.23/1.2)) = 1.6 V typ, and about 12 x (1 - e^(-0.42/1.2)) = 3.5 V worst case. Mostly safe, but it reads as a false current spike. (b) A sustained fault (latched overtemperature, overvoltage, or repetitive current limiting on a stalled pump) lets the pin settle at the 9-12 V Thevenin level, held only by the ESP32 clamp diode at roughly (9..12 - 3.6) / 12 k = 0.45-0.7 mA of injected current. ESP32 does not spec injection current, so add a Schottky (e.g. BAT54) from the ADC node to 3V3, or a 3.0-3.3 V zener to GND, so the pin never sees > VDD + 0.3 V (datasheet VIH max). Firmware should treat a reading pinned near full scale as "driver fault", not as a large current. — basis: [BTS7960 datasheet Sec. 4.3 and 4.4.7](https://www.robotpower.com/downloads/BTS7960_v1.1_2004-12-07.pdf), [ESP32 datasheet Table 5-3](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32_datasheet_en.pdf)
- PWM averaging: IS sources current only while the high side conducts. In a forward drive at duty D (RPWM PWM, LPWM low), the active half-bridge's HS conducts for about D x T, and freewheeling is via the low sides (no sense). The RC-averaged IS is therefore about D x I_motor / k + leakage, i.e. proportional to the average supply current, not the motor current. The plan's assumption "the 10 kOhm + 100 nF filter averages the 20 kHz PWM to supply current" is correct in principle. Numbers: tau = 10 k x 100 nF = 1 ms, so 20 kHz ripple is attenuated about 2 pi x 20 kHz x 1 ms = 126x (-42 dB), and the 1-1.5 % step settles in about 5 tau = 5 ms. Caveats: (1) at 20 kHz the period is 50 us, and HS on/off delays of 1.2-4.5 us (unspecified IS mirror settling on top) skew the effective duty by several us, a large relative error below about 20-30 % duty; (2) the 2 kOhm load is in parallel with the RC, so the averaging node is the 100 nF through 10 k, fine; (3) at low duty the average falls into the ESP32's <100 mV dead zone (see Q3).
- Offset: IISH up to 200 uA (typ 1 uA) is about 0-0.4 V on 2 kOhm. Measure the zero-current reading per unit with the HS on and the motor disconnected or stalled-free, or at least with the HS off, and subtract it. With typical parts the offset is only a few mV.
- PaddyB's two points imply an effective k of 2.9 A / (0.534 V / 1.2 kOhm) = about 6,500 and 3.34 A / (0.258 V / 470 Ohm) = about 6,100. That is 25-30 % below the nominal 8,500 (lower still if the module has its own IS-to-GND resistors in parallel), and inside the datasheet's 3-14 k window. Using 8,500 uncalibrated would under-read current by about 25 % on that unit. On the plan's 2 kOhm, that unit would give about 1.3 V at 4 A, already over the 2.5 dB 1.25 V ceiling.
- Tying R_IS and L_IS: fine for single-direction pumps. In normal drive only one HS conducts at a time. Both IS outputs add during a fault, giving up to 14 mA.
- Accuracy expectation at 1-4 A after a per-unit 2-point calibration against a DMM: perhaps +/-10-15 %, set by the ADC (+/-30 mV ~ +/-0.13 A at 2 kOhm and nominal k), k nonlinearity at low current, and duty-dependent timing error. Without per-unit calibration, +/-60 % or worse is within the datasheet limits. Use it for thresholds (dry-run = low current, stall = high current) with wide margins, not as a precise ammeter in the video.

### Gaps
- IBT-2 on-board components on the IS lines (some sources say 10 kOhm to GND per pin; one search summary said "two 10K resistors in series connected to ground" without a traceable source) could not be confirmed. No schematic with values was found in the sources fetched, and Handsontec's guide does not show it. If 10 kOhm per pin is fitted, tying both pins gives 5 kOhm in parallel with the plan's 2 kOhm, about 1.43 kOhm effective (scale x0.71). Measure the resistance from the R_IS/L_IS header pins to GND with the module unpowered before finalising the numbers.
- No datasheet figure for IS settling or response time under PWM, and no kILIS spec below 5 A.
- No practical report found giving measured IBT-2 IS accuracy at 1-4 A with an ESP32 or Arduino.

## Q5. GPIO4/T0 and other touch-pin constraints on NodeMCU-32S

### Takeaway
GPIO4/T0 is a good choice: it is not a strapping pin. Its only side constraints are an internal weak pull-down at reset and the fact that it is ADC2_CH0, so it cannot also be an analog input with Wi-Fi on. Of the ten touch pins, T1 (GPIO0), T2 (GPIO2), T3 (GPIO15) and T5 (GPIO12) are strapping pins, and T8/T9 (GPIO33/32) are the 32 kHz crystal pins. Espressif says the ESP32 touch sensor has not passed conducted-susceptibility testing, which matters next to a 20 kHz PWM motor driver.

### Cited Findings
- T0 = GPIO4 (also ADC2_CH0, RTC_GPIO10, HSPIHD, HS2_DATA1, SD_DATA1, EMAC_TX_ER). Touch map: T0 GPIO4, T1 GPIO0, T2 GPIO2, T3 MTDO/GPIO15, T4 MTCK/GPIO13, T5 MTDI/GPIO12, T6 MTMS/GPIO14, T7 GPIO27, T8 32K_XN/GPIO33, T9 32K_XP/GPIO32. — [ESP32 Series Datasheet v5.3, Table 4-5 and pin table](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32_datasheet_en.pdf)
- GPIO4 IO_MUX state at reset and after reset: "oe=0, ie=1, wpd" (input with weak pull-down). It is not listed among the strapping pins (GPIO0, GPIO2, MTDI, MTDO, GPIO5). — [ESP32 Series Datasheet v5.3, IO_MUX table and Table 3-1](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32_datasheet_en.pdf)
- "ESP32 Touch Sensor has not passed the Conducted Susceptibility (CS) test for now, and thus has limited application scenarios." — [ESP32 Series Datasheet v5.3, Section 4.9.3](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32_datasheet_en.pdf)

### Inferences
- The conducted-susceptibility note means noise on the supply or ground lines (motor PWM currents sharing a ground return with the ESP32) can corrupt touch counts. Give the ESP32 and the probe GND wire a ground return that does not carry motor current, keeping the BTS7960 power ground separate and star-connected, and filter in software (median of several reads, plus requiring N consecutive "dry" samples). N must still fit the 1 s budget.
- The probe's GND wire must be the ESP32 logic GND. If it goes to motor or power ground, PWM ground bounce couples straight into the electrode through the water.

### Gaps
- No NodeMCU-32S schematic was checked for on-board parts on GPIO4 (on most such boards GPIO4 goes straight to the header; GPIO2 often carries an LED). Verify against the board in hand.
