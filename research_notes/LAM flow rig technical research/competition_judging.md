# What LRC rewards and what judges reward in student hardware video submissions

Research date: 2026-10-02. Sources are mainly the official site lrc2026.tworks.in and its public JS bundles and API, the local Stage 2 question paper, press releases, and e-Yantra theme-book pages. LRC facts are kept separate from general best practice.

## Q1. Public information on LRC 2026 / 3.0 (rounds, judging, prizes, submission format, Stage 2 FAQs)

### Takeaway
The official site lrc2026.tworks.in (a React app whose text, rubrics and portal logic sit in public JS bundles) gives three rounds, prizes of Rs 5 L / 3 L / 1.5 L, and a weighted 8-criterion jury rubric that, going by its code, belongs to the Grand Finale. **The team portal's Hardware Hustle upload form does not match the team's plan.** It asks for three files uploaded directly: a **3-4 min "Journey Video" (MP4)**, a **10-12 min "Challenge Video" (MP4)** and a **scanned handwritten solution PDF in blue or black ink**. It has no ZIP or code field for this round, and only one submission per team, which cannot be changed after confirming.

### Inferences
- **The upload form contradicts the plan.** The plan's single 10-15 min video plus a "filled question paper" plus shared code does not match the portal. The portal wants two MP4s: a 3-4 min journey video, which the plan does not have, and a **10-12 min** challenge video, which is narrower than the paper's 10-15. It also wants a **handwritten** scanned PDF in blue or black ink, so typed answers could be non-compliant. A 10-12 min challenge video satisfies both the paper and the portal, so cap it at 12:00. Plan a separate 3-4 min journey video. Hand-write the answer paper and scan it to a single PDF, embedding graphs and screenshots as printed and pasted pages if needed; whether pasted pages are allowed is unconfirmed.
- **There is no code upload field for HH.** Because the paper says "Share the code", put a code link (GitHub or Drive) inside the handwritten PDF and on a title card in the challenge video. Set its permissions to "anyone with the link" and test it in a logged-out browser.
- **Files go to the portal, not as links.** The fields take MP4 files directly, so plan for encode time, upload bandwidth and a desktop browser; login is laptop or desktop only. No size limit appears in the frontend code, but the server may enforce one. Upload early; a long MP4 over a campus link can take a long time.
- **Submission is one-shot.** "You cannot change this after it is saved." Have a second person check all three files before pressing Confirm.
- **The deadline is not confirmed publicly.** The public API's HH window ends 2026-10-19 and the team's deadline is Sun 4 Oct 18:00 IST; no public text resolves this. The 19 Oct date may cover the whole round, including evaluation. Treat 4 Oct as binding unless the dashboard's "Submissions end" countdown says otherwise, and check that countdown, since it reads the per-team `submissionEndAt`.
- **What the judges reward.** The Grand Finale rubric is the best public signal. About 50% of the weight sits on technical execution (20), integration (15) and troubleshooting (15), with reliability "across trials" and failure recovery named explicitly. Documentation, project management (including "Version Control") and decision clarity carry 20% between them. The video should show repeated trials, a fault being found and fixed, a clear explanation of design choices, and a visible version history.
- Outside this brief's scope: the question paper writes "hydraulic residence time is given by Tr = Q/V". The standard definition is V/Q. The answer should note this politely and use V/Q.

### Gaps
- No public Stage 2 FAQ or clarification was found on the site; the round PDF brief sits behind team login. It is unknown whether the challenge video must be one unbroken take, or whether edited segments are allowed. The paper says only that Case 1 and Case 2 "should run in a single go without manual intervention", which is a process requirement, not a filming rule.
- Server-side file-size limits for the MP4 uploads are unknown.
- How the 120-mark HH evaluation splits across items is not in the frontend code.
- The jury rubric may be reused from the 2025 code base, though the "Hardware Hustle" naming and 2026 URLs suggest it is current.

## Q2. Previous editions (LRC 2024 "1.0", LRC 2025 "2.0"): rounds, problem statements, winners, what judges praised

### Takeaway
Both earlier editions ran two virtual stages, then an in-person build finale for the top 25 at or near IISc Bengaluru with T-Works. Judges scored integration, hardware and firmware execution, cost and project management, and later "performance outcomes". Public coverage is press-release level only: no public write-up was found of what the winning teams built or what judges said about them.

### Inferences
- The judging language has moved from "cost optimization / project management" (2024) to "control logic, implementation quality, performance outcomes" (2025), and in 2026 to "acquire real data, characterise system performance, and validate results through calculations, plots, and rigorous engineering interpretation". For Stage 2, measured performance backed by numbers and plots is likely to outweigh the polish of the build.
- The 2026 theme (industrial process automation, autonomous fluid systems) is new. Past editions were wafer handling (2024) and an integrated automation system (2025), so there are no past fluid-rig winners to imitate. Generic rubric alignment (integration, reliability, troubleshooting, documentation) is the safest guide.
- Both finales were scored on project management. The 2026 jury rubric gives 10% to Project Management (including version control) and 15% to Team Collaboration. Showing the team's plan, roles and a version-controlled repo in the journey video likely helps, and also prepares for the finale.

### Gaps
- No public description of the 2024 or 2025 Stage 2 (virtual) problem statements or video rules was found beyond the leftover portal strings above, and those cannot be dated with certainty.
- No winner LinkedIn posts or judge comments describing what made the winning builds stand out were found within the search budget. LinkedIn content is mostly not indexed by the search tool.

## Q3. What Lam Research emphasises in university / early-talent engagement (narrative hooks)

### Takeaway
Lam frames LRC as a talent pipeline for **systems engineering of semiconductor equipment**: cross-discipline integration of hardware, firmware and process, plus project management. The Stage 2 paper sets the rig in **fab facilities liquid handling**: bulk chemical and UPW distribution, where flow is the key process variable to measure, check for deviation, diagnose and restore. Lam's own fluids-engineering roles talk in terms of P&IDs, gas and liquid delivery, flow and pressure analysis, component selection, validation and FMEA. That vocabulary maps one-to-one onto the rig.

### Cited Findings
- LRC positioning: "systems-level challenges at the intersection of firmware, hardware, business operations, and industrial control systems. No single discipline wins here... in systems engineering, the best solutions are never built in isolation." "Participants learn the essence of collaboration, planning and execution under time constraints mirroring real world design and engineering team dynamics." "Students get the opportunity to prototype, troubleshoot, and iterate." — [lrc2026.tworks.in](https://lrc2026.tworks.in)
- Lam's stated skill set for the challenge: integrate "hardware, software, systems thinking, along with agility and project management skills to solve real world engineering problems for the semiconductor industry". Rangesh Raghavan (CVP and GM, Lam India): the challenge aims to "inspire the spirit of innovation and help foster future talent for the semiconductor industry." — [mediainfoline, Dec 2025](https://www.mediainfoline.com/brand/lam-research-announces-winners-of-annual-collegiate-challenge)
- The per-member observer rubric includes "Passion for Technology & Semiconductor Industry" (5%: Enthusiasm, Curiosity, Trend Awareness, Career Motivation) and "Integrity & Ethics" (5%: Honesty, Rule Respect). — [Observer bundle](https://lrc2026.tworks.in/assets/Observer-0710622c.js)
- Stage 2 paper framing (local PDF): "Industrial manufacturing depends on highly controlled liquid-handling systems to deliver process fluids reliably from a source or bulk supply to downstream process equipment... ultrapure fluids, process chemicals, solvents, developers, or CMP-related fluids. ### Inferences
- **Narrative hooks without buzzwords.** Speak in facilities and process-engineering terms: "the process variable is flow; we established it, measured it with a calibrated transmitter, showed it deviates when the valve or level changes, diagnosed why, and restored it." That is the paper's own task statement, and it mirrors how a BCDS or UPW loop is run (supply, measure, interlock, flush, safe shutdown). One sentence linking Case 2 (flush and line conditioning, then safe-state on source exhaustion) to how fab chemical lines are flushed and qualified is enough.
- Use the fluids-engineer vocabulary Lam itself uses: P&ID, component selection rationale (valves, tubing, sensor), flow analysis, verification against spec (3 L/min target), and a short FMEA or fault table. This lines up with jury criteria "Decision Clarity" and "Root Analysis".
- Lam uses simulation heavily in teaching (Semiverse), yet LRC Stage 2 explicitly bans simulations. The team's firmware simulator should stay out of every deliverable, as the team already plans. If the code repository is shared, label the simulator as a unit-test harness so it is not mistaken for simulated results.
- The "Integrity & Ethics / Rule Respect" and "Honesty" sub-criteria point the same way: show raw, unedited data, including imperfect runs, rather than polished numbers.

### Gaps
- No public Lam material ties LRC 3.0 specifically to Lam's fluid-delivery product teams; the link is inferred from the paper's framing and Lam job descriptions.
- The full text of the Lam fluids-engineer postings could not be retrieved; the quotes are search-engine snippets.

## Q4. Best practice for remotely judged engineering demo videos (e-Yantra and comparable competitions)

### Takeaway
e-Yantra (IIT Bombay) is the closest Indian precedent for video-judged hardware rounds. Its rule is consistent: a short, edited intro is fine, but **the demonstration must be one continuous, mounted-camera shot at real speed, with on-screen evidence (terminal or grader output) recorded together with the hardware**. Speed changes, cuts, music and flashy transitions in the demo segment are banned, and an edited run means **disqualification**. LRC has not published an equivalent rule (gap), but adopting e-Yantra's conventions is low-cost insurance and matches LRC's "no simulations, no shortcuts" stance.

### Cited Findings
**e-Yantra Robotics Competition, hardware-stage video rules (eYRC 2023-24 Hologlyph Bots, Task 6, "Submission Instructions" theme-book page as mirrored by a participant; the original is at portal.e-yantra.org/themeBook/hb/... behind login)** — [GitHub mirror of eYRC theme book](https://github.com/izzaaaatanishq/Hologlyph-Bots/tree/main/theme-book/4.%20Stage%202%20-%20Hardware/Task%206)
- Two parts: "Introduction Part" (to "understand your teams' approach and method") and "Demonstration Part" (to "evaluate your Task 6 performance").
- Opening screen shows "Team ID, College name, Theme name and Team members' names" for 3-5 s. The introduction is capped at **two minutes**: member intros, the leader on "any interesting aspect", and up to 60 s on "the challenges faced". It may cover "How did the team divide the work? Be honest..."
- "Demonstration part **MUST be a one-shot continuous video** and it should not be edited in terms of being fast forwarded or slowed down, the run time should be the same as actual run time. Teams uploading an edited video will be **disqualified** from the competition."
- "Each of the two segments must be one continuous shot... The only discontinuity allowed in your final video submission is between the video of your Introduction and the video of your theme run." Stitching intro and demo is explicitly allowed.
- A countdown goes at the start of the demo; there must be no audio or visual noise before run start or after run end.
- A **pre-recorded voice-over/narration** over the demo is required, so that "judges as well as the viewers are in sync with what the robot/drone is doing". "No video transition/s or background music in this part."
- Camera "should be mounted and not hand-held"; 8 MP or higher; "Do NOT shoot in vertical framing"; tripod; 4-5 ft from speakers; quiet, well-lit room; switch off AC or fan.
- "Keep the lighting conditions steady throughout the video. **Sudden change in light might falsely lead one to believe editing of the video and hence, disqualification.**" Avoid specular reflection.
- "The following windows need to be recorded **simultaneously**: Evaluator terminal... All the data (score, runtime) should be visible... Overhead camera feed; Actual bot in action". In other words, a picture-in-picture or composite of the data screen and the hardware, recorded at the same time.
- Upload to YouTube **unlisted**, titled with a strict convention (e.g., `eYRC-HB#9999_Task6_Original_Configuration`). Photos of results are appended at the end of the video and also included in the code ZIP, along with grader output text files and source. "e-Yantra reserves the right to disqualify any team if any foul play is suspected."
- "refrain from using any flashy transitions or graphics in this video specially in the Demonstration Part"; "ensure that the video flow and edit is crisp."

**e-Yantra simulation/software tasks** — [eLSI Sprint Task 1A submission](https://lab.e-yantra.org/elsi-sprint-01/task1/task_1a_Submission.html); [Task 1B](https://lab.e-yantra.org/elsi-sprint-01/task1/task_1b_Submission.html)
- The screen recording "must have both the Terminal Windows... and the... Scene Window Running simultaneously", with a sample frame layout given. The video goes on YouTube as **Unlisted**, named with the Team ID (e.g., `Sprint1#34_Task_1A`), and the link is pasted into the portal. "File names mentioned are case sensitive." ### Inferences
- **Recommended structure for the 10-12 min LRC Challenge Video**, combining the e-Yantra conventions with the LRC paper's "SUBMIT A" items:
  1. A 5 s title card (Team ID, team name, college, members), then about 1.5 min on the rig walk-through. The paper requires that "identification of each component" and "the direction of flow" are explained verbally, so point at each tag (TK-01, P-01, FT-01 ...) while saying the flow path.
  2. One **continuous, tripod-mounted, real-speed** take of Case 1 into Case 2 "in a single go", the paper's own requirement. The serial console or live plot (flow in mL/s and L/min, totaliser, state) should be visible in the same frame or as a simultaneously recorded screen capture. Start with a countdown or a visible clock and keep the lighting steady.
  3. Separately recorded, clearly labelled segments for calibration (bucket and scale), the valve-position sweep and the fault diagnosis, each also continuous within itself.
  4. About 1 min of results: plots with raw-data tables, error versus reference, and how the 3 L/min target was held.
  This keeps the one segment where cuts would matter, the Case 1 to Case 2 run, unbroken, while staying inside 12:00.
- Narrate over the run rather than adding music. Add burned-in captions for tags and key numbers, since judges may watch muted or at speed. Shoot landscape, not vertical, in a quiet room with fans off. Never fast-forward the main run; if a long fill must be shortened, show it in full or put a visible "x4" label on a separate, non-run segment. Whether LRC allows speed-ups at all is unknown.
- Show the on-screen data and the hardware at the same time. That is how e-Yantra proves a run is real, and it directly answers LRC's "acquire real data... validate results through... plots".
- The 3-4 min Journey Video maps onto e-Yantra's "Introduction Part": approach, honest division of work, challenges and how they were solved, and key learnings. It is also where the jury and observer criteria for teamwork, project management and "Passion for... Semiconductor Industry" can be evidenced.

- The primary e-Yantra theme-book URLs (portal.e-yantra.org/themeBook/...) are login-gated; the quotes come from a participant's GitHub mirror of the 2023-24 page.

## Q5. Common pitfalls in Indian student hardware competitions with online video rounds

### Takeaway
For LRC 3.0 Stage 2 the biggest risks are mechanical, not technical. Uploading the wrong number or kind of files (portal: 2 MP4s + 1 handwritten PDF) loses marks. So does overrunning the 10-12 min window, uploading a non-MP4 or odd-codec phone video, trying to upload a large file over a slow link near the deadline, or pressing a one-shot submit with a mistake in it. Analogous competitions disqualify for edited runs, plagiarism and unplayable files.

### Cited Findings
- LRC portal accepts only MP4 for both videos ("Only MP4 videos are allowed.", via `accept="video/mp4"` and a `.mp4` extension check). The solution must be a scanned PDF ("Handwritten solution must be a scanned PDF."). All three files are mandatory, and submission is final ("You cannot change this after it is saved."). — [LRC Team bundle](https://lrc2026.tworks.in/assets/Team-81254daf.js)
- LRC team login: "Team login is available on laptop or desktop only." — [lrc2026.tworks.in](https://lrc2026.tworks.in)
- The LRC qualifier MCQ was "Auto submitted" on tab switches (the `auto_submitted_for_tab_switches` flag) and capped at 12 minutes. This shows the organisers enforce rules automatically. — [EvaluationDashboard bundle](https://lrc2026.tworks.in/assets/EvaluationDashboard-48d6b8bf.js)
- e-Yantra: "Teams uploading an edited video will be disqualified"; "Sudden change in light might falsely lead one to believe editing of the video and hence, disqualification"; "Do NOT shoot in vertical framing"; "e-Yantra reserves the right to disqualify any team if any foul play is suspected." — [eYRC 2023-24 HB Task 6 mirror](https://github.com/izzaaaatanishq/Hologlyph-Bots/tree/main/theme-book/4.%20Stage%202%20-%20Hardware/Task%206)
- e-Yantra: "File names mentioned are case sensitive. Verify the file name before making the submission."; plagiarism software is used on all submissions, with disqualification for copies; videos must be **Unlisted** (participant note: "not Private"). — [eLSI Task 1A](https://lab.e-yantra.org/elsi-sprint-01/task1/task_1a_Submission.html); [eyrc4817 team kit](https://github.com/Saurabh0003M/eyrc4817-team-kit)
- IEEE RAS: "a video that does not play on a standard platform can be summarily rejected"; "only standard codecs allowed". — [IEEE RAS](https://www.ieee-ras.org/publications/video-submission-guidelines)
- Google Drive: after upload, a video "might still be processing so that it can be viewed on other devices or by people you shared it with"; "If the file is large, it may take awhile before your video is ready." Drive playback tops out at 1920 x 1080. — [Google Drive Help](https://support.google.com/drive/answer/2423694)
- Internshala's LRC 2026 listing gives wrong team-size information ("exactly 3 members"), which shows that third-party listings drift from official rules. — [Internshala](https://internshala.com/competitions/e-cell-iit-bombay-x-lam-research-win-₹10-lakh/)

### Inferences
- **File format.** Export both videos as H.264 video + AAC audio in an .mp4 container, at 1080p and 30 fps. Many phones record HEVC/H.265 or .mov; re-encode rather than rename. A 12 min 1080p H.264 file at about 8 Mbps is roughly 700-750 MB. A 4 min journey video at the same rate is about 250 MB. If the portal has an unpublished size cap, encode at about 4-5 Mbps (around 400 MB for 12 min), which still looks fine for a bench rig. Test-play the final files in VLC and in a browser before uploading.
- **Duration.** Keep the challenge video between 10:00 and 12:00 by the file's own duration; 12:30 risks an automated or manual reject. Keep the journey video between 3:00 and 4:00.
- **Handwritten PDF.** Use blue or black ink only, scan rather than photograph if possible, and merge into one PDF in page order with Team ID and name on every page, as the paper's footer has. Check the PDF is legible at 100% zoom. Graphs and screenshots probably have to be printed and attached, or redrawn by hand; this is unconfirmed, so ask the organisers. Put the code repository link, written legibly and also as a short URL, on the answer pages where the paper says "Share the code".
- **Code link.** If GitHub, make the repo public or add the organisers. If Drive, set "Anyone with the link: Viewer" and open it in an incognito window to verify; this is general best practice, not an LRC-specific rule. Do not move or delete files until results are out.
- **Deadline handling.** Upload at least several hours early. Portal uploads of ~1 GB over campus Wi-Fi can fail or stall near a deadline, and the submit is irreversible. Have a second person check the file list, durations and PDF page count before pressing Confirm. Check the dashboard's "Submissions end" countdown rather than relying on the public `/api/rounds` window (19 Oct) or third-party listings.
- **Visible identifiers.** Show the Team ID on the title card of both videos, on the first and every page of the PDF, and in the filenames, e.g. `<TeamID>_Challenge.mp4`, `<TeamID>_Journey.mp4`, `<TeamID>_Solution.pdf`. Evaluators work from a table keyed by team ID.
- **Honesty signals.** No simulator output anywhere; the rubric includes "Integrity & Ethics / Rule Respect". Keep the run continuous with steady lighting so it cannot be mistaken for an edit, as e-Yantra warns. Show imperfect trials and their spread rather than only the best one ("Consistency of performance across trials").

### Gaps
- No public reports (Reddit, LinkedIn, Unstop discussions) of LRC-specific submission failures were found; LinkedIn is mostly not indexed and Unstop has no LRC 2026 listing in results.
- The LRC server-side upload size limit and accepted MP4 codecs are unknown.
- No primary-source Indian-competition statistics on Drive-permission or file-format rejections were found. Those points are general best practice, not cited facts.
