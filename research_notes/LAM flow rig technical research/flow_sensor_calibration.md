# YF-S401 Flow Sensor Behaviour and Small-Scale Gravimetric Calibration Practice

## Q1. YF-S401 datasheet facts

### Takeaway
All sources agree on the nominal K-factor (5880 pulses/L, F ≈ 98·Q Hz with Q in L/min, ≈0.17 mL per pulse) and a 50% ± 10% duty cycle, but the vendor sheets contradict each other on accuracy (±2%, ±5% over 0.3-3 L/min only, ±10%), rated range (0.3-6 vs 1-5 L/min), pressure (0.35 to 1.75 MPa) and even orientation, so the datasheet is not a basis for any accuracy claim: the team's own calibration is the only defensible number.

### Cited Findings
- Manufacturer-style sheet "High Precision PVC Water Flow Sensor YF-S401" (hosted by iarduino.ru; text extracted directly from the PDF): working voltage DC 5-24 V (min 4.5 V); max current 15 mA at 5 V; load capacity ≤10 mA at 5 V; flow range **1-5 L/min**; "Water resistant 0.35 MPa"; "Water Pressure ≤1.75 MPa"; operating temperature ≤80 °C, liquid ≤120 °C; duty cycle 50% ± 10%; output high level > DC 4.7 V at 5 V input; "Flow pulse characteristics F = (98 * Q) ± 2%, Q = L/MIN"; a separate "Caution" line says "Frequency: F = 90 * Q (L/Min) Error: ±2%, voltage: 3.5-24VDC, current can not exceed 10mA" (internally inconsistent: 98 vs 90); "Error: +/-2L/min" (garbled); "Internal diameter: 1.2mm" (implausible, likely typo); "Install it in vertical, inclination should not beyond 5 degree"; intended for "water dispenser or coffee machine". — [iarduino YF-S401 datasheet PDF](https://iarduino.ru/lib/2377bf7550064248856024f6728d7cc3.pdf)
- DFRobot SEN0216 (Gravity board around YF-S401): range 0.3-6 L/min; "1L = 5880 square waves"; accuracy "±5% (0.3-3L/min)" (no spec given above 3 L/min); proof pressure <0.8 MPa; 5-12 V; 15 mA at 5 V; output high >4.5 V, low <0.5 V at 5 V supply; duty 50% ± 10%. — [DFRobot wiki SEN0216](https://wiki.dfrobot.com/SEN0216)
- TinyTronics: range 0.3-6 L/min; accuracy **±10%**; "98 pulses per second ... for each liter of fluid passing through per minute", Q [L/min] = f [Hz]/98; 5-24 V; pulse output 4.7 V at 5 V supply; max pressure 8 bar; -25 to 80 °C; 7 mm barbs (fits 6 mm ID hose); unidirectional (arrow). — [TinyTronics YF-S401](https://www.tinytronics.nl/shop/en/sensors/liquid/yf-s401-water-flow-sensor)
- Arduino forum: user quotes a ±2% datasheet claim and "98 means around 0.17 ml for each pulse"; an experienced respondent says calibration is device- and plumbing-specific ("your plumbing equipment will effect the sensor performance"). — [Arduino forum: Flow Sensor YF-S401](https://forum.arduino.cc/t/flow-sensor-yf-s401/1254486)
- Arithmetic consistency: 98 Hz per L/min × 60 s = 5880 pulses/L; 1/5880 L = 0.170 mL/pulse. (Note: search snippets claiming "2.25 mL per pulse" for the YF-S401 are copy-paste from the YF-S201, which is 450 pulses/L.) — [DFRobot wiki SEN0216](https://wiki.dfrobot.com/SEN0216); [components101 YF-S201](https://components101.com/sensors/yf-s201-water-flow-measurement-sensor)

### Inferences
- Output type: none of the fetched sheets states "open collector" or "push-pull" explicitly. A ≥4.5-4.7 V high level at 5 V supply plus a "load capacity ≤10 mA" spec is consistent with a Hall switch with an on-board pull-up to Vcc (typical for this family), i.e. the signal swings to the sensor supply. Practical consequence for an ESP32: if the sensor is fed 5 V (or 12 V, which the 5-24 V rating allows), the signal line will sit at ~5 V (or 12 V) and must be divided/clamped to 3.3 V; do not rely on the ESP32 internal pull-up alone. This should be verified on the bench with a multimeter/scope (measure idle-high voltage with the yellow wire unloaded).
- Accuracy for the report: quote "vendor-stated accuracy ranges from ±2% to ±10% depending on source" rather than one number; the team's measured calibration error supersedes it.
- At 5 L/min the nominal frequency is ~490 Hz; at 1 L/min ~98 Hz. A 40 s run at 1 L/min yields ~3900 pulses, so ±1 count quantisation is ~0.03%: pulse counting resolution is never the limiting factor.
- The 0.35 MPa "water resistant" line is the most conservative pressure figure; a 12 V diaphragm sprayer pump typically has a pressure switch around 0.4-0.8 MPa (not verified here), so the sensor should be on the low-pressure return side (as planned) and never dead-headed.

### Gaps
- No primary manufacturer (e.g., the YF/"Sea" factory) English datasheet with a flow-vs-frequency table was found; the iarduino PDF is the closest to a factory sheet and is internally inconsistent.
- No source explicitly documents the internal pull-up resistor value or whether the output is open-collector-with-internal-pull-up vs push-pull.

## Q2. Measured K-factor spread and linearity (YF-S401 and siblings)

### Takeaway
The YF-S401's pulses-per-litre is not constant: one published bench calibration (datalogd) gives K rising from ~5140 p/L at 1 L/min to ~5735 p/L at 5 L/min (about 10% spread, all below the 5880 datasheet value), but almost all of that nonlinearity is an offset, so Q = a·f + b with a non-zero intercept fits 1-5 L/min to about ±1.2% with R² ≈ 0.9999 on that curve, whereas a through-origin fit (Q = f/K) would be ~9% off at 1 L/min. A linear fit with intercept, restricted to ≥1 L/min, is therefore realistic; extending it below ~0.5-1 L/min is not.

### Cited Findings
- datalogd (open-source Python data logger by P. Tapping) ships an experimentally fitted YF-S401 calibration: counts-per-litre K as a function of counts-per-minute x, K(x) = a(1 − exp(−k(x − x0)^b)) with a = 5975, k = 0.173734, x0 = 0, b = 0.284333; code computes Q[L/min] = cpm / K(cpm). The docstring states plainly that "the pulse rate will not be directly proportional to the flow rate (each pulse does not correspond to a fixed volume of liquid)", and says the defaults "may be compatible" with YF-S402 / YF-S402B. A coffee-machine sensor FM-HL3012C gave a = 1950, k = 0.0965882, b = 0.721649. — [datalogd docs](https://datalogd.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/datalogd.plugins.flowsensorcalibration_datafilter.html); [datalogd source](https://datalogd.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/datalogd/plugins/flowsensorcalibration_datafilter.html)
- Vendor note on the sibling YF-S201: "not a precision sensor, and the pulse rate does vary a bit depending on the flow rate, fluid pressure and sensor orientation"; needs careful calibration "if better than 10% precision is required" (quoted from search-index summary; the page fetch returned empty). — [Altronics YF-S201](https://www.altronics.com.au/product/j0085-yf-s201-hall-effect-water-flow-sensor)
- A 2017 IEEE ICCE-Asia paper (Lalnunthari & Thanga, "Dependence of hall effect flow sensor frequency on the attached inlet and outlet pipe size", DOI 10.1109/icce-asia.2017.8307842) reports, per its search-indexed summary, that as the attached pipe becomes smaller than the sensor's internal diameter, the slope of the calibration curve decreases and the intercept increases, i.e. the calibration depends on the tubing attached. — [IEEE Xplore 8307842](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8307842/) (abstract not retrievable directly; summary via search index)
- Academic YF-S201 studies (search-index summaries; full texts on ResearchGate were 403-blocked): one reports slope/R² between sensor and reference of S = 0.9983, R² = 0.9986 and S = 0.9921, R² = 0.997; another reports "accuracy of 97% with a maximum discrepancy of 3%". — [ResearchGate: A Calibration Technique for Water Flow Sensor YF-S201](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/376751071_A_Calibration_Technique_for_Water_Flow_Sensor_YF-S201_1); [ResearchGate figure: YF-S201 flow results](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/The-results-of-flow-measurement-by-the-YF-S201-sensor_fig7_357044012)
- Vietnamese journal paper on YF-S401 in a drink mixer (Hoàng Mạnh Kha et al., 2021, Tạp chí KH&CN ĐH Công nghiệp HN, vol. 57 no. 4) improved accuracy with output-noise suppression plus sensor calibration; numbers only in the Vietnamese PDF. — [VJOL article page](https://www.vjol.info.vn/index.php/dhcnhn/article/view/62213)
- Arduino forum: calibration factor must be found per device and per plumbing; pulsating piston/diaphragm pump flow flagged as "probably not" suitable for accurate measurement. — [Arduino forum](https://forum.arduino.cc/t/flow-sensor-yf-s401/1254486)

### Inferences
- Evaluating the datalogd YF-S401 curve numerically (my calculation from the cited parameters): Q = 0.3 → f ≈ 22 Hz (K ≈ 4410 p/L, 73.5 Hz per L/min); Q = 0.5 → 40 Hz (K ≈ 4750); Q = 1 → 86 Hz (K ≈ 5140, 85.7 Hz per L/min); Q = 2 → 182 Hz (K ≈ 5460); Q = 3 → 280 Hz (K ≈ 5600); Q = 4 → 379 Hz (K ≈ 5680); Q = 5 → 478 Hz (K ≈ 5735, 95.6 Hz per L/min).
- Least-squares Q = a·f + b through 5 points at 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 L/min on that curve gives a = 0.01019 L/min per Hz (1/a = 98.1 Hz per L/min, i.e. the datasheet "98" behaves like the incremental slope, not the ratio f/Q), b = +0.138 L/min, R² = 0.99996; residuals +1.19%, −0.42%, −0.32%, −0.06%, +0.18% of reading; max error over a dense 1-5 L/min sweep 1.19% (at 1 L/min, systematic curvature). A through-origin fit gives −9.2% at 1 L/min and +1.2% at 5 L/min. Adding a 0.5 L/min point to the fit pushes the error at 0.5 L/min to +4.6%.
- Consequence for the plan: R² ≥ 0.995 and max error ≤ 3% over 1-5 L/min are consistent with this sensor's intrinsic curvature, provided the intercept b is fitted (not forced to zero) and the lowest point is not much below 1 L/min. The real risk to the 3% target is not the shape of the curve but run-to-run scatter from the start/stop method and pump pulsation (Q3, Q5). Expect the lowest-flow point to have the largest residual, and expect b > 0 (the rotor needs some flow before it turns: a positive intercept is physical, not an error).
- Sensor-to-sensor and installation dependence (tube ID 5 mm vs barb bore, orientation) means the datasheet K and the datalogd curve are only priors; the team's own fit is the result. A measured slope within roughly ±10% of 98 Hz per L/min would be unsurprising; a much larger deviation suggests air, a wiring/counting problem, or a mis-scaled flow.

- No source quantifies YF-S401 cut-in (minimum starting) flow; the datasheet lower limit of 0.3 L/min (or 1 L/min in the iarduino sheet) is the only indication.

## Q3. Pulsating flow (diaphragm pumps) effects on small turbine/rotor meters

### Takeaway
Rotor meters over-register the mean flow in pulsating flow because the rotor speeds up faster than it slows down; for small (6-25 mm) liquid turbine meters this stays within normal accuracy for pulsation below ~10 Hz but reached +5.5% at higher pulsation frequency/amplitude. A diaphragm sprayer pump pulses at its stroke frequency (likely tens of Hz or more), so the bias is plausible at the few-% level, but because the team calibrates in situ against weighed mass with the same pump and plumbing, any steady pulsation bias is absorbed into a and b; what is not absorbed is a pulsation level that changes with operating point or between calibration and use.

### Cited Findings
- Brunel PhD thesis "Dynamic response of small turbine flowmeters in pulsating liquid flows" (Betty Lee, 2002), meters 6-25 mm, pulsation up to 300 Hz: below 10 Hz the errors were within meter accuracy; at higher frequencies and larger amplitudes "the largest over-registration observed was 5.5 %" and "amplitude attenuation could be as large as 90 %"; the cause is "the fundamental asymmetry between flows with increasing and decreasing angle of attack relative to the meter blades", giving positive error in mean flow (over-registration) and underestimated pulsation amplitude; error depends primarily on the velocity amplitude of the pulsation at the rotor. — [Brunel BURA 2438/4971](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/handle/2438/4971?mode=full); [thesis PDF](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/4971/4/FulltextThesis_Chapters_1-7.pdf)
- US patent 5,481,924 addresses assessing and quantifying "pulsation induced error in gas turbine flow meters", confirming pulsation error is an established turbine-meter effect (gas context). — [US5481924](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5481924)
- Arduino forum respondent: centrifugal, vane or peristaltic pumps are suitable for these sensors; "pulsating flow from a piston or diaphragm pump probably not". — [Arduino forum](https://forum.arduino.cc/t/flow-sensor-yf-s401/1254486)

### Inferences
- Because the 5.5% figure scales with velocity amplitude at the rotor, the mitigations are the ones that cut amplitude at the sensor: (1) an air chamber/accumulator (a capped vertical tee or small sealed bottle with trapped air) between pump and sensor; (2) compliance from a length of soft PU tubing upstream of the sensor (8x5 mm PU is fairly compliant, which already helps); (3) a flow restriction (needle valve) downstream of the compliance to form an RC low-pass; (4) placing the sensor far from the pump. Averaging the pulse count over a long window removes jitter but not the over-registration bias.
- Since the mass reference is pulsation-blind and the pump is the same in calibration and use, a constant bias becomes part of the fitted slope. Risk cases: if flow is set by changing pump voltage/PWM, the stroke frequency and amplitude change with flow, so the bias varies with operating point and can show up as curvature or larger residuals; if an accumulator is added or removed after calibration, the calibration is no longer valid. Rule: freeze the hydraulic configuration before calibrating.
- A cheap diagnostic: log individual pulse intervals (ESP32 micros()) at fixed flow; a strong periodic modulation at the pump stroke frequency indicates large pulsation at the rotor. Comparing pulses/kg with and without an air chamber at the same mean flow quantifies the bias directly.

### Gaps
- No source found giving the stroke/pulsation frequency of 12 V knapsack-sprayer diaphragm pumps or quantifying pulsation error specifically for YF-series plastic rotor sensors.
- The ASGMT paper on pulsation effects (asgmt.com/054.pdf) was 403-blocked.

## Q4. Installation effects

### Takeaway
Calibration is only valid for the installation it was done in: orientation, the tube attached to the barbs, entrained air and back-pressure all shift the rotor's K. The practical rules are: keep the sensor full and bubble-free (bleed air, flow upward if vertical, sensor not at a high point), keep its orientation and the attached tubing identical between calibration and use, put any throttling valve downstream, and keep straight tube at the inlet where possible.

### Cited Findings
- YF-S401 factory-style sheet: "Install it in vertical, inclination should not beyond 5 degree" (no straight-run requirement given). — [iarduino YF-S401 datasheet PDF](https://iarduino.ru/lib/2377bf7550064248856024f6728d7cc3.pdf)
- Sibling YF-S201 vendor note: pulse rate varies "depending on the flow rate, fluid pressure, and sensor orientation" (quoted in search-index summary of vendor pages; page fetch returned empty). — [Altronics YF-S201](https://www.altronics.com.au/product/j0085-yf-s201-hall-effect-water-flow-sensor)
- IEEE ICCE-Asia 2017 (Lalnunthari & Thanga): Hall flow sensor calibration depends on the attached inlet/outlet pipe size; when the attached pipe is smaller than the sensor's internal diameter, slope decreases and intercept increases (per search-index summary). — [IEEE Xplore 8307842](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8307842/)
- Turbines Inc. LF-series low-flow turbine meter manual: meters are calibrated horizontally and "it is recommended that the meter is installed in the same (horizontal) orientation", though they "may ... be operated in any orientation"; must be installed in arrow direction; should "remain full of line fluid even when no flow occurs"; "air should be bled from the line fluid prior to start up"; over-speed "usually occurs during startup when air is present in the metering line"; a minimum back-pressure prevents two-phase flow and cavitation, BP = 2·ΔP_meter + 1.25·VP; "Throttling valves should be installed downstream of the turbine flow meter only"; operating below the minimum rated flow "will cause the performance to become non-linear"; "Severe pulsation of flow will affect the accuracy of the flow meter". — [Turbines Inc. LF Series manual](https://testing.instrumart.com/assets/turbines-inc-lf-series-low-flow-turbine-meter-Manual.pdf)
- ICS Schneider (turbine meter troubleshooting): "If air or gas content is carried along, the rotor is not driven evenly"; inlet disturbances "can cause the turbine to rotate faster or slower than expected"; "pressure and flow pulsations can become visible as an apparently changing flow rate"; "Below the minimum flow rate, the flow energy may not be sufficient to drive the rotor stably. The measured value can then be too low, fluctuate or drop out completely." — [ICS Schneider: turbine meter shows wrong values](https://www.ics-schneider.de/messturbine-zeigt-falsche-werte-ursachen-bei-einbau-medium-und-kalibrierung/?lang=en)
- General turbine-meter guidance (search-index summary only; the PDF could not be downloaded to confirm, so treat attribution as unverified): at least 10 diameters straight upstream and 5 downstream, increased to 20 upstream directly after a valve or centrifugal pump; in vertical runs flow should preferably pass upward so entrained bubbles clear quickly. — [Bell Flow Systems BLFT datasheet (unverified)](https://www.bellflowsystems.co.uk/files/attachments/377/BLFT%20Data%20Sheet_2022.pdf)

### Inferences
- For 5 mm ID tubing, 10D/5D is only 50 mm / 25 mm of straight tube: trivially achievable, so give the sensor ≥5-10 cm of straight tube on each side and avoid a kink or tee right at the inlet barb.
- The YF-S401 barbs (~7 mm OD per TinyTronics) are meant for ~6 mm ID hose; 8x5 mm PU will be a tight stretch fit. The 5 mm ID is close to the barb bore, so the pipe-size effect should be small, but the calibration must be done with the exact tube and fittings used in the final rig.
- Air: a diaphragm pump drawing from a reservoir can entrain air if the suction line or return splashes; bubbles under-drive the rotor (under-registration) while the mass still counts, which shows up as scattered, low K. Prime and run the loop for a minute before each calibration set, keep the return outlet submerged or at least not aerating the suction side, and orient the sensor so bubbles are swept out (flow upward if vertical, per the YF datasheet's vertical-mount note).
- Back-pressure: on the return line the sensor is near atmospheric pressure, so cavitation is unlikely at 1-5 L/min water; the main back-pressure issue is that the outlet height/tube length set the hold-up volume (see Q5), so fix the outlet geometry.
- If flow is set with a valve, put the valve downstream of the sensor (per LF manual); if set by pump voltage, see Q3 on pulsation changing with operating point.

### Gaps
- No quantified data on how much YF-S401 K changes between horizontal and vertical mounting.
- The IEEE pipe-size paper's numbers were not accessible.

## Q5. Gravimetric calibration: standing start vs flying start, error sources, uncertainty, reporting

### Takeaway
The team's method is the standard "standing start and stop" gravimetric method (flow stopped at both ends, total pulses vs total collected mass), which is legitimate (ISO 4185 covers weighing methods) but whose dominant error is the start/stop transient (meter response and changing flow at each end), not the scale. With a 1 g scale and ≥1 kg collections the weighing contribution is ≤0.1%, timing is irrelevant if pulses and mass are compared directly, and the realistic error budget is set by per-run transients, drain-down/hold-up and drips, which are fixed amounts per run and therefore matter most for the shortest, lowest-flow runs. An ISO 4185-style "one long run vs n short runs" test quantifies this per-start/stop error directly.

### Cited Findings
- Two gravimetric approaches: standing start-and-stop (flow fully stopped at start and finish) and flying start-and-stop (steady flow switched into/out of the weighing tank by a diverter, timer triggered by the diverter). Diverter methods "are used primarily for flowmeters with slow response times and where flow rate is the primary measurement rather than quantity passed." — [Sârbu, IMEKO TC4 2017, Evaluation of Measurement Uncertainty in Calibration Standard Gravimetric Installation](https://imeko.org/publications/tc4-2017/IMEKO-TC4-2017-093.pdf)
- "The main source of uncertainty for gravimetric calibration methods lies in the timing error; in the case of the standing start and stop method, this is caused by the response time of the flowmeter and the changing flow at each end of the test; for the diverter methods, this is introduced by not triggering at the hydraulic center of the liquid jet." — [Sârbu, IMEKO TC4 2017](https://imeko.org/publications/tc4-2017/IMEKO-TC4-2017-093.pdf)
- Weighing-method uncertainty components (ISO 4185 / GUM / EA-4/02 practice): balance linearity, balance calibration uncertainty (from its certificate), temperature drift of the balance, balance resolution with standard uncertainty u_res = d/(2√3) (rectangular), air-buoyancy correction, evaporation (only relevant for hot water), leakage (assigned 0.03% rectangular if no correction made); volume is obtained by dividing buoyancy-corrected mass by water density at the meter. Top facilities reach expanded uncertainty ~0.02-0.03% (k = 2) with ≥1000 kg collections. Flow instability is checked as max-minus-min indicated flow over ≥5 min at each test flow. — [Sârbu, IMEKO TC4 2017](https://imeko.org/publications/tc4-2017/IMEKO-TC4-2017-093.pdf)
- PTB (Cordova & Lederer, FLOMEKO 2013): the timing/diverter error matters "only ... if long measurement times are not possible"; measurements "taking less than one minute and where an expanded uncertainty of under 0.1% is aimed should be generally analyzed in detail"; diverter travel takes "from a few milliseconds up to one second". ISO 4185 gives an empirical test: compare one regular collection (one filling interval) with a collection split into n filling intervals of the same total; the difference gives the constant time error added per diverting action, Δt ≈ [(Σm_i/Σt_i0)/(m/t0) − 1]·t0/(n−1) (paraphrased, see paper eq. 3). PTB's measured diverter-related contributions were ~0.008-0.016%. — [Cordova & Lederer, PTB, FLOMEKO 2013](https://www.imeko.org/publications/tc9-2013/IMEKO-TC9-2013-013.pdf)
- Reference standard: ISO 4185, "Measurement of liquid flow in closed conduits — Weighing method"; ISO 5168 for uncertainty; GUM; EA-4/02 for expression of uncertainty in calibration. — [Sârbu, IMEKO TC4 2017 reference list](https://imeko.org/publications/tc4-2017/IMEKO-TC4-2017-093.pdf)

### Inferences (worked for this rig)
- What the team actually measures: per run, N pulses and collected mass m over the same pump-on window. The meter factor K_run = N/V with V = m/ρ needs no clock at all, so timing error cancels in K. Flow rate Q_run = V/t and f_run = N/t share the same t, so a timing error shifts a point along the fitted line rather than off it (to first order). A clock is only needed to assign Q and f; ESP32 millis() timing (crystal, ~±50 ppm typical, not verified here) is negligible.
- Scale resolution: 1 g resolution gives u_res = 1/(2√3) = 0.29 g per reading; net mass is a difference of two readings (tare and gross), so u ≈ 0.41 g, i.e. 0.04% of 1 kg and 0.009% of 4.5 kg. Kitchen-scale linearity/calibration (typically several grams at a few kg; check with a known mass such as sealed 1 L bottles or gym weights) will dominate the weighing term, probably 0.1-0.3%, still small against the 3% target.
- Air buoyancy: weighing water against steel-calibrated scale under-reads true mass by ≈ ρ_air(1/ρ_w − 1/ρ_ref) ≈ 1.2(1/998 − 1/8000) ≈ 0.105% (standard buoyancy formula; computed). Either apply +0.1% or state it as an uncorrected 0.1% systematic.
- Standing-start errors, per run, roughly fixed in absolute size, so their relative effect is ∝ 1/(run volume):
  (a) Pump ramp-up/ramp-down: during the ~0.1-1 s spin-up/coast-down, flow passes through the low-flow region where the rotor slips (K is lower, possibly zero below cut-in), so the meter under-registers that volume. If ramp volume is ~1-2% of a 40 s run and K there is 20-50% low, the bias is ~0.2-1% and is worst at low flow (same absolute ramp, smaller total).
  (b) Rotor spin-down after pump stop: rotor inertia keeps generating pulses after flow stops (over-registration). Count until pulses cease (e.g., stop counting 1-2 s after the last pulse), not at the pump-off instant.
  (c) Line hold-up / drain-down: the return tube from the sensor to the bucket holds water; if it is full at start and drains (or partially drains) into the bucket after stop, the bucket gets volume the meter never saw (or vice versa). 8x5 mm tube holds π/4·(5 mm)²·L ≈ 19.6 mL per metre, i.e. 0.4-2% of a 1-4.5 kg collection per metre of tube. Rule: start and end every run with the outlet line in the same state (e.g., outlet held at a fixed height so it stays full, or always fully drained), and wait a fixed time for drips before reading.
  (d) Drips/splash and wet-bucket tare: tare the bucket wet (after a previous run's drain) or dry consistently; a wetted 10 L bucket can hold several grams of film water.
- Practical check modelled on ISO 4185's timing-error test: at one flow, compare one long run (e.g., 180 s) against three back-to-back 60 s runs of the same total; the difference in pulses-per-kg divided by the extra number of start/stops gives the per-start/stop error directly. If it is <0.3% for the 40 s runs, the 40-90 s run length is adequate; if not, lengthen low-flow runs or switch to a flying start (divert the outlet hose into/out of the bucket by hand while the pump runs and gate the pulse count on the same action).
- Run length vs the plan: at 1 L/min a 40 s run collects only ~0.67 kg (below the plan's ~1 kg) and the fixed per-run errors above are relatively largest there; at 5 L/min a 54 s run already gives 4.5 kg. Suggest sizing runs by mass (≥1 kg, preferably ≥2 kg) rather than time, which means ~60-120 s at 1 L/min.
- Points and repeats: an industrial low-flow turbine meter's standard factory calibration uses 20 data points across its range with water ([Turbines Inc. LF manual](https://testing.instrumart.com/assets/turbines-inc-lf-series-low-flow-turbine-meter-Manual.pdf)). 5 flows × 3 repeats (15 runs) is a reasonable student-scale design (my judgement, no standard consulted for the exact count): 5 points are enough to see curvature in the residuals, and 3 repeats give a first repeatability estimate. Repeatability per point = standard deviation (or range) of the 3 runs; with n = 3 the SD estimate is itself uncertain by ~50% (see Q6), so report it but do not over-interpret it. If time allows, a 6th point near 0.5-0.7 L/min, or one independent validation run, adds more than extra repeats at existing points.

### Gaps
- ISO 4185 text itself (paywalled) was not consulted; its procedure is cited via PTB/IMEKO papers.
- No published source was found that quantifies standing-start error specifically for small hobby rotor meters; the per-run numbers above are engineering estimates.

## Q6. Is R² an appropriate acceptance metric? Better metrics

### Takeaway
R² is a weak acceptance metric for a meter calibration: it is driven by the spread of flows (1-5 L/min) and is insensitive to percentage errors at the low end. For this 15-run design, R² ≥ 0.995 is met even when individual runs scatter by 2-3% of reading (max errors of 4-6%), so the binding criterion is the per-point % error of reading, not R². Report R² because the brief asks for it, but lead with residuals in % of reading, the standard error of estimate, per-point repeatability, and an uncertainty statement.

### Cited Findings
- Logue (2018, Talanta 189:527-533): "R² has well-known disadvantages when used to quantify GOF of calibration curves stemming from its calculation based on the absolute error of the signal"; "R² did a poor job of consistently and accurately quantifying the GOF over the entire calibration curve", especially when low-end calibrators are poorly described; proposes Percent Residual Accuracy (%RA), which equally weights all calibrators. — [Logue, Percent Residual Accuracy (SDSU Open PRAIRIE)](https://openprairie.sdstate.edu/chem_pubs/77)
- Coleman & Vanatta, "Statistics in Analytical Chemistry Part 44: R²" (American Laboratory, Oct 2011): R² is a "bulk property" that cannot separate model inadequacy from noise; in their example the dataset with highest R² had the worst quality, and a low-R² set reached 0.9999 with a better model while revealing a low-end defect; they recommend residual-pattern analysis and a lack-of-fit p-value. — [American Laboratory Part 44](https://americanlaboratory.com/913-Technical-Articles/35232-Part-44-R-sup-2-sup/)
- Restek / analytical-method practice (search-index summary): several recent US EPA methods (533, 537.1, OTM-45) dropped the r² requirement in favour of measuring the error at each calibration point; "1% off at 100 ppm is the same as 100% off at 1 ppm" as far as r² is concerned. — [Restek article](https://discover.restek.com/es/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/51753)
- Flow-meter specs distinguish % of full scale (FS: fixed absolute error, so the % error grows at low flow; e.g. 1% FS of 200 = 2 units, which is 2% at half flow) from % of reading (Rd: same % everywhere); at low flow a % Rd meter can be up to ten times more accurate than a % FS one. Repeatability (same result on repeated runs under identical conditions) is typically much tighter than accuracy (e.g. ±0.1% vs ±1%). — [Bronkhorst: flow meter accuracy & repeatability](https://www.bronkhorst.com/int/blog-1/flow-meter-accuracy-repeatability/)
- Turbines Inc. LF manual: using one average K over the range gives "an error approximately equal to the linearity error"; linearization with a meter-factor curve (K at each flow) reduces it; their standard calibration uses 20 data points over the range with water; repeatability spec ±0.25% of reading. — [Turbines Inc. LF Series manual](https://testing.instrumart.com/assets/turbines-inc-lf-series-low-flow-turbine-meter-Manual.pdf)

### Inferences (worked for this plan)
- Monte-Carlo on the planned design (my calculation: 5 flows × 3 repeats at 1-5 L/min, random error ε of reading per run, fit Q_ref = a·f + b): ε = 0.5% → median R² 0.99989, median max |error| 1.0%; ε = 1% → R² 0.99957, max |error| 1.95% (95th percentile 3.0%); ε = 2% → R² 0.9983, max |error| 3.9% (95th pct 6.0%); ε = 3% → R² 0.9961, max |error| 5.9%. So R² ≥ 0.995 is satisfied by a calibration that would fail the ≤3% max-error target; meeting max |error| ≤ 3% reliably needs per-run scatter of about 1% (1σ) or better. This makes run-to-run repeatability (Q5 standing-start errors, Q3 pulsation, Q4 air) the real acceptance risk.
- Recommended reporting set (all cheap to compute from the 15 runs): (1) a, b with their standard errors (from the regression); (2) residual per run as % of reading, e_i = (Q_fit,i − Q_ref,i)/Q_ref,i × 100, plotted against Q (look for curvature: a U-shape means the line is the wrong model, see Q2); (3) maximum |e_i| and mean |e_i| (state which: mean absolute error, not mean signed error, since signed residuals of a least-squares fit average to ~0 by construction); (4) standard error of estimate s = √(Σ(Q_fit − Q_ref)²/(n − 2)) in L/min; (5) repeatability per flow point = SD of the 3 repeats in % (and note n = 3 gives a very uncertain SD: GUM's Table E.1 gives ~52% relative uncertainty for an SD from 3 readings); (6) R², as requested. Optionally a validation run at a flow not used in the fit (e.g., 2.5 L/min) reported as "independent check error", which is far more convincing than any in-sample statistic.
- State errors as % of reading (the natural unit for a K-factor meter) and, if one number is wanted, also as % of the 5 L/min span; do not mix them.
- Uncertainty statement (simple GUM-style, k = 2): combine in quadrature the reference uncertainty (scale resolution and linearity, density, buoyancy, hold-up/drip estimate) and the fit's standard error of estimate at each point; expanded U = 2·u_c. On this rig the reference terms are ~0.1-0.5% and the fit/scatter term will dominate, which is itself a credible finding to report.

## Q7. Water density at 20-30 °C and its effect

### Takeaway
Pure water density is 998.2 kg/m³ at 20 °C, 997.0 at 25 °C and 995.7 at 30 °C, so converting grams to millilitres as 1 g = 1 mL understates the true reference volume by 0.18-0.44% (it biases the fitted slope a by that amount; per-point residuals are barely affected because a constant scale factor is absorbed by the fit); the temperature sensitivity is only ~0.026% per °C, so a single water-temperature reading per session is enough. The effect is small versus a 3% target but is a free correction that signals rigour in the report.

### Cited Findings
- Engineering ToolBox table (1 atm): 4 °C 999.97; 15 °C 999.10; 20 °C 998.21 (0.9982067 g/cm³); 25 °C 997.05 (0.9970470 g/cm³); 30 °C 995.65 (0.9956488 g/cm³) kg/m³. — [Engineering ToolBox: water density](https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/water-density-specific-weight-d_595.html)
- Gravimetric practice: volume is obtained by dividing the buoyancy-corrected mass by the water density at the meter, computed from temperature (and pressure). — [Sârbu, IMEKO TC4 2017](https://imeko.org/publications/tc4-2017/IMEKO-TC4-2017-093.pdf)

### Inferences
- Values from the CIPM/Tanaka et al. (2001, Metrologia 38:301) pure-water formula (my computation; matches the ToolBox values at 20, 25 and 30 °C to 0.01 kg/m³): 21 °C 998.00; 22 °C 997.77; 23 °C 997.54; 24 °C 997.30; 26 °C 996.79; 27 °C 996.52; 28 °C 996.24; 29 °C 995.95 kg/m³. Slope ≈ −0.26 kg/m³ per K near 25 °C (−0.026%/K): a ±2 °C uncertainty in water temperature is ±0.05% in volume.
- Combined with the ≈ +0.10% air-buoyancy correction (Q5), the correct conversion is V[L] = m_scale[kg] × (1 + 0.00105) / (ρ(T)/1000). At 28 °C (plausible for an Indian lab in early October) the net correction vs "1 kg = 1 L" is about +0.48% in volume.
- Tap water with typical dissolved solids is very slightly denser than pure water; the difference is negligible relative to the other terms (not quantified here; no source fetched).
