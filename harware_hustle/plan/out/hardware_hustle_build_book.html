Objective

By Sunday 4 October, 18:00 IST, upload the three files the LRC portal requires: a 10–12 minute Challenge Video (MP4), a 3–4 minute Journey Video (MP4) and the solution handwritten in blue or black ink, scanned to one PDF that also carries the ISA-5.1 P&ID, the labelled photos, the plots and the code link. Together they must show a leak-free, ESP32-automated rig that runs Case 1 into Case 2 in one go, holds 3.0 L/min on an FT-01 calibrated in place by weighing, and stops within about one second of the flush tank running dry.

Done when

The single-take run (switch press → Case 1 with the external-fluid event → automatic Case 2 → automatic stop) passes three rehearsals in a row and is on video. Calibration maximum error ≤ 3% with repeats within ±1%, and R², a, b and average error reported. Every Task 1–3 question has measured data and a graph.

Not doing
  • Wi-Fi, web or cloud dashboards. ADC2 dies with Wi-Fi on, and USB is safer on camera.
  • A machine-learning fault classifier. There is no training data, and a rule table is explainable.
  • Touching the FT-01 spool after calibration, or any tubing that arrives after Friday.
  • The pressure transducer: ordered on Friday it arrives too late to install and validate.
  • Rework of the custom PCB. All additions go on a small add-on board.
Constraints that bind
  • About 2.5 build days. One rig and one scale are shared by everyone.
  • Kit plus passives on hand. Online parts take 1–3 days, so they can only be upgrades.
  • Pumps cut out at 8.25 bar. FT-01 is rated 0.8 MPa, and the PU tube and push-fits about 0.8–1 MPa.
  • The portal takes exactly three files, all required, MP4 or PDF only, and none can be changed after saving. It has no field for code, photos or the P&ID, so they go inside the PDF and the video.

Sheet 00 · Strategy

How we place top 3

Hardware Hustle sends 25 of its 50 teams to the Grand Finale. Top 3 means doing everything the round asks, visibly, with nothing missing, and then showing judgement that other teams do not.

What this round rewards, in its own words

“Using the provided kit, teams must design, build, test, and validate a working physical system – no simulations, no shortcuts. Teams will acquire real data, characterise system performance, and validate results through calculations, plots, and rigorous engineering interpretation.”

Grand Finale judges evaluate “system architecture, component selection, control strategy, implementation, optimisation, troubleshooting, and overall engineering execution”. Lam describes the challenge as integrating “hardware, software, systems thinking, agility, and project management”.

What loses points

  • A required answer missing or vague: no Q2.3 table, no R², no Q3.2 for every condition.
  • A demo with a hidden hand: anything touched in Cases 1–2 other than BV-01.
  • Plots without units, captions or interpretation; screenshots unreadable at 1080p.
  • Simulated, estimated or invented numbers presented as data. The round says “no simulations”: every submitted number comes from the rig.
  • Leaks, dangling wires or wet electronics anywhere in frame.
  • Buzzwords without evidence (“AI”, “smart”, “state of the art”). Name the method and show its result.
  • A Challenge Video outside 10–12 minutes, no Journey Video, a typed paper, or a file the portal rejects (only MP4 and a scanned PDF). Weak audio, or a component never named.
  • No code link in the PDF and on the video, although the paper asks for the code and the portal has no field for it.

Sheet 01 · Ground truth

What we found, and the decision each finding forces

Each finding is checked against a primary source or the kit itself. If a finding turns out to be wrong at the bench, that is a replan trigger (sheet 10).

The LRC team portal's Hardware Hustle form asks for three uploads, each required: a Journey Video (MP4, 3–4 min, “your team's efforts and prototype development journey”), a Challenge Video (MP4, 10–12 min) and a Handwritten Solution (scanned PDF, “written using blue or black ink”). →

Keep 4 Oct as the deadline unless the dashboard countdown or the organisers say otherwise.Source: LRC portal, team submission form (JS bundle) · LRC rounds API
A 12 V sprayer diaphragm pump is not a flat positive-displacement source. A comparable 3-chamber pump (Shurflo 8000-543-238) gives 6.8 L/min open, 5.0 at 2.8 bar and 3.6 at 6.9 bar, while its current rises from 3.0 to 8.7 A. Scaled to our 6.1 L/min pump, P-02 at 100% reaches 3.0 L/min only at about 7 bar: near its 8.25 bar switch and the tube's rating.
→
Map P-02 at 100% against the BV-02 dial with IT-02 at M2. If the 3.0 L/min trim makes the switch cycle, hold 3.0 L/min with BV-02 open and a reduced d2 instead, and show the data. Keep the 100% sweep as the Q2.1 evidence.Source: Shurflo 8000-543-238 data sheet · Shurflo 8000 series tables
FT-01 is a YF-S401: 5,880 pulses per litre (F = 98·Q), 0.3–6 L/min, ±10% uncalibrated, output high above 4.5 V on a 5 V supply, rated 0.8 MPa.
→
Level-shift the pulse to 3.3 V before G14. Mount FT-01 after BV-02 so it never sees the pumps' 8.25 bar cut-off. Calibrate it in place.Source: TinyTronics YF-S401 · BC Robotics YF-S401
The paper's “8 mm pipe” is the tube's outside diameter. The kit tube is 8 mm OD × 5 mm ID, so 3 L/min runs at 2.55 m/s (Re ≈ 12,700), not 0.99 m/s, with about 19 kPa of friction per metre.
→
Measure the bore with calipers on day one and use it in Q1.2. Expect the loop's maximum to fall short of 6 L/min, and explain why with the pump and system curves.Source: Kit list, item 1 (PU tube 8 mm OD × 5 mm ID)
SOV-01's label reads 0–10 kg/cm², so it is a zero-differential (direct-acting) valve and will open on P-01's suction side. ¼-inch valves of this class (e.g. 2W025-08) have a 2.5 mm orifice (Cv ≈ 0.23), which costs about 0.8 bar at 3 L/min (1.3 bar at Kv 0.16), all of it on the suction side. Raising TK-01 by 0.3 m adds only 0.03 bar, so it helps priming, not this loss. P-01 may cavitate somewhere near 1.5–2.5 L/min at any duty.
→
Test P-01's flow through SOV-01 in hour one and use the strongest pump as P-01. If 50% duty cannot carry 3.0 L/min, raise the duty and show the data (3.0 L/min wins). If no duty reaches 3.0, run Case 1 on sump inventory with the planner's settings (sheet 03), so FT-01 still holds 3.0 L/min.Source: STC 2W025 data (Cv 0.23)
Each IBT-2 IS pin has 10 kΩ to GND (5 kΩ when the two are tied), giving 0.59 V/A nominal. The BTS7960 sense ratio spreads from 3,000 to 14,000, so a pin can see 8 V. The 74HC244 buffer powered at 5 V needs about 3.5 V for a logic HIGH.
→
Add 3.3 kΩ (about 2 kΩ total, 0.24 V/A), then 10 kΩ in series and 100 nF at the pin, with a small Schottky (BAT85) from the pin to 3V3 so a driver fault (4–7 mA) cannot push the pin above 3.3 V. Calibrate each channel against the DMM. The 2.5 dB range ends at 1.25 V, and one measured IBT-2 (k ≈ 6,100) reaches it at about 4 A. Keep every channel under 1,100 mV at P-02's highest-pressure point. Power the IBT-2 VCC from 3.3 V.Source: DCC-EX IBT_2 current-sense notes · BTS7960 data sheet · Espressif ADC ranges
Rotor meters read high in pulsating flow (up to +5.5% in small liquid turbine meters), and the error changes with the pulsation's frequency and amplitude. A hall flow sensor's frequency also depends on the pipe attached to it and on its orientation.
→
Calibrate with P-02 pumping through the final installed spool, so the pulsation and plumbing are the real ones. Freeze that spool afterwards. Verify at the exact Case 1 setting (P-02 duty and BV-02 position), because the calibration runs vary the duty and so change the pulsation.Source: EFRC guideline, pulsating-flow meter errors · Brunel thesis, turbine meters in pulsating flow · IEEE 8307842
Static weighing (ISO 4185) is a primary method for calibrating flow meters, provided the line runs full. In start-stop runs the scale is not the problem (1 g is 0.04% of 2.5 kg). The errors are fixed amounts per run: pump ramp and rotor slip at each end, drips, and water held in the outlet tube. They weigh most in short low-flow runs. R² ≥ 0.995 can still pass with 4–6% point errors, so it is a weak acceptance test.
→
Calibrate by weight on the 1 g scale, with each run at least 1.5 kg. The ESP32 times each run itself, so the pulse count and the collected mass share one window. Accept on maximum error and repeatability, and report R².Source: ISO 4185 preview · IMEKO TC9 2013, start-stop flow calibration · datalogd, a measured YF-S401 curve
ESP32 touch sensing works through a plastic wall, but the threshold is specific to each placement and the reading drifts. A foil on the wall also trips while water still covers the pickup, so on camera the system would stop with the tank visibly unfinished.
→
Make LSL-03 a bare-wire probe on the touch pin, taped at the dip-tube inlet. It goes dry exactly when P-03 can no longer draw. Re-capture the wet reading every run. P-03's current then separates “empty” from “BV-01 still shut”, and a timeout backs both up.Source: Random Nerd Tutorials, ESP32 touch · Maakbaas, touch as a level sensor
ADC2 cannot be read while Wi-Fi is on, and only G36 and G39 are free ADC1 pins on this PCB.
→
Keep Wi-Fi off and stream telemetry over USB to the laptop console. G15 (ADC2) carries the third current channel.Source: Espressif ADC documentation

Harness, in build order

  1. Continuity map before power: each ESP32 socket pin to its connector pin. Confirm whether SW and FSW are G34 and G35 or swapped.
  2. Driver harness from U2/U3/U4 to the IBT-2s: GND, L_EN, R_EN, LPWM, RPWM. Leave U-header pin 1 (5 V) unconnected and feed IBT-2 VCC from 3V3.
  3. On each IBT-2, tie R_IS to L_IS and add 3.3 kΩ to GND. Run a twisted pair (IS plus GND) to the add-on board.
  4. Solder G36, G39, G15, G4 and G5 to the underside of the socket pins (socket pins 3, 4, 23, 26, 29). Mark them with sleeves.
  5. Put a flyback diode (1N4007 or 1N5819) across the SOV coil: band to +12 V.
  6. On the add-on board, clamp each IS pin (G15, G36, G39) to 3V3 with a BAT85 or BAT43, band to 3V3. A driver fault turns IS into a 4–7 mA source that would otherwise drive the pin towards 12 V.
  7. Mount LS-01 so that contact OPEN = level HIGH. A broken wire then also reads high and stops P-01.
  8. Make the LSL-03 probe from two bare tinned wires 5 mm apart, one to G4 and one to GND. Tape them along the TK-03 dip tube so 5 mm of bare wire ends 2–3 mm above the inlet. Twist the lead and keep it away from the motor wires.

Electrical safety, non-negotiable

  • Bond the SMPS PE terminal and close its terminal cover. Have a qualified person check the mains side, as the kit sheet requires.
  • Power-off checklist (sheet 08) before every first power-up. Check polarity at the PCB input with the DMM.
  • Keep pump wiring (16/18 AWG) apart from signal wiring. Twist the FT-01 lead and route it away from the motor leads.
  • Never hot-plug the motor leads. Never run a pump against a closed valve; I-1 to I-4 back this up in firmware.
  • Power-up order: 12 V on, ESP32 boots with every driver input LOW, then USB console. The firmware drives all driver pins LOW in its first statement.

Calibration is ISO 4185 static weighing. The ESP32 starts and stops P-02 itself and counts every pulse until the rotor stops, so f = N/t and Q = V/t share one exact window. Those are the paper's own formulas, without stopwatch error. Calibrate in place, through the final FT-01 spool, with the pump's real pulsation.

Procedure (about 75 min for 15 runs)

  1. Freeze the FT-01 spool: no tube moves near the meter from now on.
  2. Lay the L-202 end into a bucket on the scale, falling continuously so it drains the same way every time. Tare.
  3. Pick five duties from the duty map to give about 1, 2, 3, 4 L/min and the maximum. Do 3 runs each. Nothing below 1 L/min: the rotor slips there, and a point at 0.5 L/min alone adds about 5% error to the fit.
  4. Console: CAL 60 35 (seconds, duty %). Size runs by mass, at least 1.5 kg: 120 s below 1.5 L/min, 40 s above 4 L/min, and each run under 4.5 kg. Log the water temperature.
  5. Wait for the CAL_DONE event, which comes once the rotor has stopped. Weigh, enter the net grams below, and pour the water back into TK-02. Shake the bucket to the same wetness each time and re-tare.
  6. Start-stop check, once at 3 L/min: one 180 s run against three 60 s runs. If they differ by more than 0.5%, the per-run start and stop error is too big: lengthen every run.
  7. Paste the SET lines into the console, then SAVE. Verify at the exact Case 1 setting (P-02's d2 and BV-02 on its trim mark); it must agree with the scale within ±2%. If it misses, add three runs at that setting to the fit and report the difference: it is the pulsation effect.

What good looks like

  • Maximum error ≤ 3% of reading and repeats within ±1% of each other: that is the gate. Report R² as well, but do not accept on it; 2–3% scatter still gives R² ≥ 0.995.
  • On the certificate: a and b with their standard errors, the residual of every run in % of reading, maximum and mean absolute error, repeatability at each flow, and the expanded uncertainty (k = 2) at 3 L/min.
  • K within ±10% of the datasheet's 5,880 pulses/L. A larger miss means air in the meter or a cracked rotor seat.
  • A positive offset b is physical: the rotor needs a minimum flow to turn. The firmware zeroes Q below fcut, so b never adds to the totaliser at rest.
  • Pulse duty on the console ≈ 50 ± 10%. Any other value means a magnet or wiring fault.

Q3.1 residence time

Q3.1 written answer

τ = V/Q = 0.5 L ÷ 2.5 L/min = 0.2 min = 12 s. The sheet prints Tr = Q/V; that ratio, 5 min−1, is the turnover rate, and residence time is its inverse.

  1. Meaning: the average time a fluid parcel spends in that section. It is also the time to displace the section once, and the transport delay between something entering and it reaching the meter. Turbulent flow behaves close to plug flow.
  2. V doubled: τ doubles, to 24 s.
  3. Q doubled: τ halves, to 6 s.
  4. Why it matters: in a full, incompressible line, a change in pump or valve shows up at FT-01 almost at once, because pressure waves travel at hundreds of m/s. Anything carried by the fluid (an air slug, the flush front, a contaminant) arrives τ later. A tank with a free surface breaks the chain: on our rig the flush tank running dry is invisible at FT-01 until the sump drains. So we detect it at the source (LSL-03, IT-03). Residence time also sets alarm delays and the dead time a controller must tolerate.

Bonus insight: the sump is a mixed tank. Pushing 4.2 L through about 2.5 L of mixed liquid leaves e−4.2/2.5 ≈ 19% of the original fluid behind. Lower the sump first, or flush longer.
